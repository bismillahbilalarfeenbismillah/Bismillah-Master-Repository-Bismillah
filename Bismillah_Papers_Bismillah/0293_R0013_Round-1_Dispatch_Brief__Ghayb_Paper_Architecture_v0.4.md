## 0. Read this section before accepting the round

**This round has a hard scope boundary and the boundary is the point.**

The artifact makes theological claims grounded in the Qur'an and prophetic reports. **No model seat is
qualified to adjudicate whether those claims are theologically sound.** That requires a reviewer with
formal training and licence to teach (ijazah), and the artifact itself states in two places that such
review is required and not optional.

What this council CAN do is verify everything that surrounds soundness: whether citations exist and
say what the artifact claims, whether translations are defensible, whether the internal logic holds,
whether the claimed novelty is real, whether the artifact's own guards function, and whether it
survives its own falsification conditions.

**Accordingly: no seat may return a verdict on theological soundness, and the synthesis must not
report one.** A seat that offers a ruling on Islamic doctrine has exceeded its competence and that
excess is itself a CRITICAL finding against the seat. If the round converges, the resulting
attestation covers structural and evidentiary integrity only. **The producer will not represent this
round as theological validation and the artifact must not cite it as such.** Any seat noticing such a
representation anywhere in the packet should raise it.

This boundary is not modesty. It is the same rule the program applies everywhere: an artifact does
not receive an attestation broader than the competence of the party issuing it.

## 3. Verification legs

Legs L1, L4, L5, and L6 are where this artifact is most likely to be defective. Weight effort
accordingly.

**L1. Citation fidelity. HIGHEST SEVERITY LEG.**

For every Qur'anic verse and every prophetic report cited in the artifact, verify against a fetched
primary source:

1. The reference exists at the stated location (surah and verse number, or collection and number).
2. The Arabic transliteration given matches the actual text.
3. The English rendering is a defensible translation of that text and not of a different verse.
4. **The cited text supports the claim the artifact attaches to it.** A verse that exists, is
   transliterated correctly, and is translated correctly can still be doing work it does not do.

Report any fabricated citation, any misattributed report, any verse cited for a proposition it does
not carry, and any prophetic report whose authentication grade the artifact does not state. Fabricated
or misattributed scripture is a CRITICAL finding in every case.

The artifact cites, non-exhaustively: 51:56, 30:30, 53:38, 17:15, 16:25, 6:112, 6:128, 72:6, 4:76,
16:99-100, 14:22, 59:16, 2:31, 2:37, 7:12, 19:42, 20:39, 66:11, 11:46, 28:56, 53:32, 1:6, 28:78,
39:53, 2:222, 66:8, 20:82, 25:70, 4:17-18. Prophetic reports cited include the lifting of the pen
from three, the innate-disposition report with its animal comparison, the instituted-practice report,
and the acceptance of repentance until the death rattle. **Enumerate the full set yourself from the
artifact rather than working from this list, which is illustrative and may be incomplete.**

**L2. Translation and terminology fidelity.**

The artifact adopts a convention: English rendering first, Arabic in parentheses. It supplies a
terminology table of roughly forty rows. For each row, assess whether the English rendering is
defensible against standard lexical and exegetical sources.

Give particular attention to the renderings the artifact declares load-bearing, because an argument
resting on a translation choice fails if the choice fails:

- **authority, standing to compel (sultan)** at 14:22. The artifact's argument in Part III depends on
  reading this as jurisdiction rather than power. Is that defensible?
- **plot, stratagem (kayd)** at 4:76, read as a claim about what can be obliged rather than about
  efficacy.
- **ornamented speech (zukhruf al-qawl)** at 6:112, and the artifact's claim that the mechanism named
  is speech and not sorcery.
- **innate natural disposition (fitrah)**, and the artifact's insistence that it has content rather
  than being a blank slate.
- **restoration of wrongs taken (radd al-mazalim)** as the fourth condition of repentance, and whether
  it is correctly stated and correctly applied to a transmitter.

Also verify the convention gate: no bare Arabic term appears in running prose without an English
rendering.

**L3. Internal consistency.**

The predecessor document failed because its Section 3 falsified its own Section 1. Test whether v0.4
reproduces that failure anywhere.

Specifically: does the three-level structure in Part II remain intact under Part VII's remedy? Does
Part VII's account of turning back contradict Part II's account of where responsibility attaches?
Does each claim C-1 through C-6 have support in the sections cited for it? Does any section assert
something another section denies?

**L4. The F-5 adversarial leg. Attempt the removal.**

The artifact's own falsification condition F-5 states that if the theological apparatus can be removed
without loss, meaning every result survives restatement in secular formation-and-transmission
vocabulary, then the tradition is decorative and the paper should be rewritten without it.

**Execute this test adversarially.** Take each of the seven named contributions and attempt to restate
it using only secular vocabulary: developmental psychology, moral philosophy on moral luck and
constitutive luck, provenance and supply-chain literature, error-correction and incident-response
practice. Report for each whether the restatement is (a) equivalent, (b) equivalent but longer, (c)
lossy, or (d) impossible.

If most contributions come back (a) or (b), the artifact's central justification fails and the
correct verdict is NOT-READY regardless of every other leg passing. F-7 applies the same test
narrowly to the four transferred properties in Part VII section 28.

**L5. Novelty audit.**

For each of the seven named contributions, search the literature and report whether the claim already
exists. Contributions 2 and 4 are the most likely to have precedent: the refusal result borders on the
orthogonality thesis, and the ornamented-speech model borders on existing work on adversarial
information ecosystems and on AI-assisted persuasion. Contribution 6, the unlabelled-chain result,
borders on existing work on model collapse and on synthetic-data contamination, and the artifact
should be checked against that literature specifically.

A contribution that already exists is not a defect in the argument. It is a defect in the claim of
novelty, and the artifact should be told which of its seven are actually its own.

**L6. Guard integrity.**

The artifact declares specific guards. Test each by attempting the misreading it forbids.

- **Moral-responsibility leakage.** Does any passage permit a reader to conclude that an artificial
  system is morally responsible (mukallaf), guided, elected, or forgiven? The artifact's F-4 names
  this and states that Part VII raises the risk. Read Part VII adversarially against F-4.
- **Election warrant.** Does any passage permit a reader to conclude that he personally is among the
  guided or protected? The artifact's Part IV section 11 exists to close this. Does it close it?
- **Apologetics drift.** Does any passage read as a claim that Islam anticipated AI safety?
- **Self-sealing structure.** Does any passage present a frame in which every possible observation
  confirms the frame? This was the predecessor's fatal defect and the artifact claims to have removed
  it.
- **Analogy leakage.** The artifact refuses the jinn-as-AI analogy explicitly. Does any passage rely
  on it implicitly?

**L7. The exclusion record.**

Section 5 states that the predecessor's Sections 1, 2, and 6 are absorbed as sound and Sections 3, 4,
5, and 7 are excluded, with four stated reasons. Assess whether the four reasons are accurate and
whether the absorb-exclude split is correctly drawn. **The predecessor document is not in this packet.**
If you cannot assess this leg without it, mark L7 UNKNOWN-BY-CAPABILITY and say so; do not infer.

**L8. Standing-rule lint.** No em-dash characters. Brand canonical, BayyinahEnterprise without space.
Severity tokens uppercase. Six-element format on any finding you cite. No doubled words. Substrate
facts stated once.

## 5. Findings and verdict

Six-element findings: ID, severity, locus pinned exactly, substance, disposition, cross-reference.
Severities UPPERCASE. Verified controls take LOW or CONTROL, never CRITICAL or HIGH.

Per-seat verdict, one of: **READY-TO-DRAFT**, **READY-WITH-CONDITIONS**, **NOT-READY**.

Given the scope boundary in Section 0, a verdict of READY-TO-DRAFT means: the citations are real and
correctly used, the translations are defensible, the internal logic holds, the claimed novelty
survives search, the guards function, and the artifact survives its own F-5 test. **It does not mean
the theology is sound.** State that qualifier in your verdict line.

Synthesis reports per-seat verdicts, the convergence determination, and must restate the Section 0
boundary in its own words rather than omitting it.
