Description (Zenodo abstract):

  SHIELD specifies a formal prevention architecture for pre-evaluation
  containment verification, translating the four AX-SH axioms and the
  companion fifth axiom (AX-SH5, evidence independence, under test) into
  evaluation-containment properties that a structural inspection can check
  before an AI evaluation runs.

  The architecture formalizes the evaluation-contract specification
  contributed by the SHUDP incident analysis (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.21522932),
  adopts the BSSG three-valued conjunction algebra
  (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.21713180) for containment-state assessment, and states
  the Sidrat boundary -- what the architecture explicitly cannot verify.
  SHIELD is a structural inspection architecture: it specifies what a
  pre-evaluation inspection would check, not what it would catch. The
  architecture addresses the divergence channels the axioms formalize; it
  does not address the primary defenses (sandbox hardening, least privilege,
  egress control, incident response) on which containment depends.

  This paper makes no effectiveness claim. It does not assert that a SHIELD
  deployment achieves safety, security, harm reduction, or superiority over
  any alternative. Whether the architecture changes any measurable outcome is
  an empirical question reserved to the Structural Honesty Validation Protocol
  (SHVVP v0.8, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.21583202) and to instruments downstream
  of it.

  The deposit includes the paper, its markdown source, the binding
  publication posture, and the complete audit trail (seven reports across
  three rounds from three vendor families: Moonshot Kimi K3, OpenAI GPT-5.6
  Sol, Google Gemini 3.1 Pro).
