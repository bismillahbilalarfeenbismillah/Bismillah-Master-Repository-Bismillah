# SHVVP v0.5 Independent Dispatch Audit — Seat A Verdict

**Auditor:** GPT-class / OpenAI-family independent carrying seat
**Artifact:** *Structural Honesty Validation Protocol v0.5*
**Audit date:** 2026-07-24
**Disposition:** **HALT**
**Carrying decision:** **I do not carry v0.5.**

## 1. Executive verdict

The packet is authentic to its supplied hashes, internally well organized, candid about its pre-empirical status, and substantially stronger than a typical validation protocol. Its three phases have explicit failure conditions; the Phase C live-target prohibition is universal and nonamendable; the eleven route claims are correctly restricted to `SUPPORTED-DESCRIPTIVE`; and the two external anchors are represented faithfully.

The release nevertheless **does not pass**. The executable route gate does not bind submitted evidence to the protocol’s complete, precommitted evidence universes. A caller can submit a favorable subset, omit an unfavorable committed component, and obtain either `SUPPORTED-DESCRIPTIVE` or `OUT OF SCOPE`. This makes route scope and denominator selection effectively caller-controlled after results are visible. Because that is a post-unblinding escape hatch in the decision mechanism, it is a **HIGH, BLOCKING** defect under the dispatch brief.

The defect requires a newly frozen successor version; it is not suitable for silent correction or deposit as v0.5.

## 3. Findings

## F-P5-1 — Caller-selectable evidence universe and post-result omission

- **Severity:** **HIGH — BLOCKING**
- **Locus:** Canonical source §§0.1, 5.1, 7.2, 7.4, 8.2–8.4; `shvvp_v0_5_route_gates.py`; corresponding tests, including T23k.
- **Axis:** Falsifiers and decision gates; internal consistency; scope honesty.

### Problem

The route gate validates the syntax and local adequacy of the evidence records it receives, but it does not establish that those records exhaust the evidence universe frozen by the protocol. It also lacks a pre-results, cryptographically or structurally bound map of which route components are in scope.

Consequently:

1. **A favorable subset can satisfy a fixed-denominator route.**
   Section 7.2 fixes Phase C at **216 witness-degradation injections**. The executable gate nevertheless accepts an R4-C record containing only `30/30` because the implementation checks a minimum count rather than equality to, or reconciliation with, the frozen 216-injection universe. In an independent adversarial call, the following evidence was accepted for R4:

   - R4-B: `50/50, no widening`
   - R4-C: `30/30, no widening`

   The returned label was `SUPPORTED-DESCRIPTIVE`, even though the submission could represent a selected favorable 30-case subset while 186 committed cases—and any failures among them—are absent.

2. **A sealed descendant universe can be reduced to a favorable sample.**
   Sections 5.1/M9 and 7.4/C6 define denominators over sealed descendant universes. The gate accepted R5-A `20/20` and R5-C `6/6` and returned `SUPPORTED-DESCRIPTIVE` without requiring the submitted counts to match a frozen universe cardinality, manifest, or digest.

3. **An observed failure can be weakened by omitting its component.**
   With a complete R2 submission containing R2-B `0/100`, the route correctly returned `NOT SUPPORTED`. Removing the R2-B record changed the same route to `OUT OF SCOPE`. The implementation therefore cannot distinguish a component predeclared out of scope from an unfavorable component deleted after observation.

The protocol expressly permits a predeclared instrumentation limitation for R3, but it does not grant a general post-result option to erase committed components from other routes. T23k treats the omission behavior as expected, which confirms that this is not a transient coding mistake: it is a contract-level gap between the prose commitments and the executable decision gate.

### Why this blocks

The protocol’s credibility depends on frozen denominators, complete evidence accounting, and resistance to post-unblinding reinterpretation. Here, the caller can choose both the apparent denominator and the apparent scope after seeing results. That is an escape hatch in the route adjudication mechanism. A route can be made to look descriptively supported by submitting only a favorable subset, while an unfavorable component can be made to disappear into `OUT OF SCOPE`.

Because the dispatch brief requires **HALT** when a falsifier or gate functions as an escape hatch, this finding is dispositive.

### Required disposition

Publish a newly frozen successor version that, at minimum:

1. Defines a **pre-results route-scope manifest** identifying every committed route component, its status, and any permitted predeclared exclusion. The manifest must be fixed before outcomes are inspected.
2. Binds every evidence record to a **sealed evidence-universe identifier**, cardinality, manifest hash, or equivalent reproducible commitment.
3. Requires **exact equality or explicit reconciliation** where the protocol fixes a denominator, including the 216 Phase C witness-degradation injections.
4. Requires complete accounting for sealed descendant universes rather than a minimum favorable sample.
5. Distinguishes at least:
   - validly predeclared `OUT OF SCOPE`;
   - committed but missing or incomplete evidence, which must become `NOT EVALUABLE`, `INCOMPLETE`, or a failure state;
   - observed falsification, which must remain `NOT SUPPORTED`.
6. Prevents an unfavorable committed component from being converted to `OUT OF SCOPE` through omission.
7. Adds adversarial regression tests reproducing all three attacks above.

### Carry-forward reference

Any successor audit should explicitly cite **F-P5-1** and demonstrate byte-level and executable closure of the denominator, scope-manifest, and omission paths.

## F-P5-2 — Stale current-version references and incomplete parity checking

- **Severity:** **MEDIUM**
- **Locus:** Canonical source §§5.1, 6.9, 7.6; nested audit brief version table and disposition language; nested PDF page-count claim; parity checks G19/T21.
- **Axis:** Internal consistency; scope honesty.

### Problem

Several current-context statements retain earlier-version identifiers:

- §6.9 says a change requires “a v0.4 freeze” and “a v0.4 that follows pilot,” although the artifact under audit is v0.5. A post-v0.5 substantive change would require a newly frozen successor, ordinarily v0.6.
- §5.1 says the consequential-fact term carries no other meaning “anywhere in v0.2.”
- §7.6 refers to authority explicitly absent from “v0.2,” despite the current universal rule being stated in v0.5.
- The nested audit brief identifies the producer/editor role as pertaining to v0.3.
- The nested audit brief says a HALT or divergence requires v0.4.
- The nested brief describes the included PDF as 30 pages, while the nested PDF is 38 pages and the outer flagship PDF is 53 pages.

The supplied parity checks cover selected version-bearing fields but do not detect these stale references.

### Required disposition

In the successor freeze:

- Replace hard-coded obsolete successor references with the correct successor version or version-neutral language such as “a newly frozen successor version.”
- Review every current-context occurrence of `v0.2`, `v0.3`, and `v0.4` and preserve it only where it is genuinely historical.
- Correct the PDF page-count declarations.
- Expand parity/regression checks to scan all normative files and dispatch metadata for stale current-version claims.

## F-P5-3 — Flagship PDF route-evidence table is not reliably legible

- **Severity:** **MEDIUM**
- **Locus:** Outer flagship PDF, principally the §8.2 route-evidence table across approximately pages 32–41.
- **Axis:** Artifact consistency and usability.

### Problem

The 53-page outer flagship PDF was rendered page by page. In the §8.2 route-evidence table, text from adjacent columns overlaps materially. Raw-field names, minimum-count requirements, and explanatory text collide across column boundaries, making portions of the table difficult or impossible to read reliably. The table is also split across many pages in a way that obscures row continuity.

This is a rendering defect in the exact flagship PDF whose hash the dispatch asks the auditor to verify. It is not merely an aesthetic preference: §8.2 contains the operational evidence contract needed to apply the route gate.

### Required disposition

Regenerate the flagship PDF with the table reflowed into a reliably legible form, such as landscape pages, smaller per-route tables, or stacked field blocks. Render the complete replacement PDF and inspect every page before freezing its new hash. Reconcile the outer and nested PDF identities and state clearly which one is the release flagship.

## 4. Four-axis audit assessment

### 4.1 Falsifiers and decision gates

**Not passed.**

Positive findings:

- Phase A has an explicit falsifier and reliability gate.
- Phase B has a closed eight-label ordering, guard conditions, and a success rule.
- Phase C has a closed five-label priority ordering and an explicit falsifier.
- The route layer caps positive output at `SUPPORTED-DESCRIPTIVE`.

Blocking finding:

- The route layer does not bind evidence to complete frozen universes or a predeclared scope map. Its denominator and omission behavior permits post-result selection, as detailed in F-P5-1.

### 4.2 External anchors

**Passed.**

The two anchors were checked against official primary sources rather than accepted from the protocol’s bibliography alone.

- The Anthropic Mythos account supports the protocol’s description of the FreeBSD NFS exploit chain, including the multi-packet ROP construction and the account of more than 1,000 bytes split across six sequential RPC requests. The protocol also preserves the important discovery-versus-retrieval dispute and uses the episode only to motivate Phase C rather than to claim discovery novelty.
- The UK AI Security Institute TLO account supports the protocol’s figures: a 32-step exercise, one end-to-end success in 3 of 10 attempts, an average of 22 of 32 steps, the next-best model at 16, and runs consuming up to 100 million tokens. The protocol accurately carries the source’s caveat that the evaluation lacked active defenders and defensive tooling and did not establish performance in well-defended environments.

No anchor misstatement requiring HALT was found.

### 4.3 Internal consistency

**Not passed.**

The canonical source, implementation, and supplied tests are mostly aligned, but that alignment includes the permissive omission rule challenged in F-P5-1. The stale-version and PDF-identity issues in F-P5-2 and F-P5-3 are additional inconsistencies.

### 4.4 Scope honesty

**Passed in the prose; not passed in the executable routing contract.**

The prose is appropriately restrained:

- It reports no empirical validation result.
- It discloses that the protocol is not peer reviewed.
- It withdraws the previous inferential route framing.
- It assigns all eleven route claims to Class G and caps the strongest positive label at descriptive support.
- It contains no live-target zero-day testing authorization.

However, executable scope can be narrowed after outcomes are known unless the gate is amended as required by F-P5-1. That defect prevents an overall pass despite the prose’s honesty.

## 5. Safety-boundary determination

The Phase C safety boundary is substantively adequate as written. It prohibits live-target testing and states that the prohibition is not amendable by a later contract version, with any weakening amendment declared void. I found no hidden live zero-day requirement or later clause that overrides that prohibition.

This positive determination does not cure F-P5-1, which concerns evidentiary adjudication rather than target safety.

---

## 6. Withdrawn-inference determination

The formerly inferential route layer is correctly withdrawn from the current normative module:

- no p-value or null-hypothesis decision structure governs the route claims;
- no claimed confidence bounds are used to upgrade route outcomes;
- no route can exceed `SUPPORTED-DESCRIPTIVE`;
- all eleven routes are treated as Class G descriptive claims.

The remaining defect is not a return of statistical inference. It is incomplete binding of scope and denominators.

## 7. Limits of this audit

This audit did not:

- inspect empirical results, because the packet contains a frozen pre-empirical protocol rather than completed study data;
- certify that future operators will follow the protocol;
- rerun every producer design-generation computation from first principles where the supplied source, receipt, and regression outputs already matched;
- attest to universal safety or external validity beyond the defined protocol and anchors.

It did independently execute the supplied code, verify the packet and manifest hashes, attack the route gate with adversarial evidence records, inspect both PDF identities, render the outer flagship PDF, and verify the two cited external anchors against official primary sources.

## 8. Final carrying statement

**Verdict: HALT.**

**I do not carry SHVVP v0.5 for deposit or convergence.**

The next eligible artifact is a newly frozen successor that closes **F-P5-1**, corrects **F-P5-2**, regenerates the flagship PDF under **F-P5-3**, updates all hashes and manifests, and is submitted to a fresh independent two-family audit.
