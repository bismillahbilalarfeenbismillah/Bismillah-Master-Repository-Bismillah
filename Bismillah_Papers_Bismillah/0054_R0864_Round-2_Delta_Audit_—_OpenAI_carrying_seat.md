### L4. Citation fidelity on imported sources

On NIST AI 600-1, the paper is substantively right that the relevant transparency stack is not one thing: NIST distinguishes provenance data tracking from synthetic-content detection, and within provenance tracking it separately names watermarking and metadata recording techniques. [NIST AI 600-1](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf)

On C2PA, the paper is also substantively right: the explainer describes Content Credentials as securely bound provenance assertions and tamper-evident under alteration, while the official FAQ explicitly says manifests are typically embedded but can be separated and that metadata may be stripped, with soft bindings used as a recovery mechanism. [C2PA Explainer](https://spec.c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/1.4/explainer/Explainer.html) [C2PA FAQ](https://c2pa.org/faqs/)

On the EU AI Act, however, the paper overstates Article 50. Article 50(2) does require synthetic outputs to be marked in a machine-readable format and detectable as artificially generated or manipulated, but the article does **not** itself impose lifecycle logging obligations. [EUR-Lex Regulation (EU) 2024/1689](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32024R1689)

That overstatement is not in the main body language at the insertion point; it appears in the empirical-and-standards source note, which currently says Article 50 is cited both for machine-readable marking and for lifecycle logging obligations on covered systems. [Current paper source](file:///home/user/workspace/uploaded_attachments/7dc1167c4d504e07a260721d02ca5533/paper_source-4.md) [EUR-Lex Regulation (EU) 2024/1689](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32024R1689)

On watermark robustness, the new text is fair in aggregate. Paraphrasing weakens watermark strength even when some schemes remain detectable with enough tokens, translation-based attacks can drive detection toward random-guessing levels, low-entropy instruction responses are harder to watermark robustly, and published attacks have removed several schemes with only minor quality degradation. [On the Reliability of Watermarks for Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04634) [Can Watermarks Survive Translation?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14007) [Robust Distortion-free Watermarks for Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15593) [Watermarks in the Sand](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04378)

On Shumailov and the qualification literature, the paper’s use is fair to both halves. Shumailov et al. do support the claim that recursive training on generated data shrinks distribution support and loses tails first, while follow-up work does support the narrower qualification that collapse depends strongly on whether real human data are retained or accumulated alongside synthetic data. [Shumailov et al. via PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11269175/) [Is Model Collapse Inevitable?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.01413) [How Bad is Training on Synthetic Data?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05090)

### L5. The bridge principle, adversarially

The bridge principle is much better than Round 1, but it still overstates two things.

First, condition 4 is framed too narrowly if it is part of the **exact applicability** test rather than the remedy test. A decommissioned synthetic corpus, benchmark, or archived model-output artifact can still propagate formative harm downstream even if the original producing system no longer has a live present-tense action locus. In those cases, the relevant correction point sits with the current custodian, deployer, or ingester, not necessarily with the originating system.

Second, the sentence claiming that conditions 1 and 3 are decidable from public facts does **not** survive real deployment opacity as written. In openly documented pipelines this can be true, but in ordinary frontier deployments outsiders often cannot tell, from public facts alone, whether outputs are being re-ingested into later training or evaluation loops.

Those are not fatal objections to the bridge principle itself, but they do require narrowing edits before circulation.
