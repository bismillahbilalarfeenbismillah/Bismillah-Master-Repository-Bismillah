## Comprehensive analysis

The high-confidence result is that the v0.9 repair pass succeeded on its principal targets. All three seats independently reproduced the substrate hashes, verified both corrected Zenodo correspondences, confirmed the METR failed-hack and self-restoring-hook language, located the severity-anchor and formal-language repairs, and reproduced the required references-before-footer build. The independent council build also showed clean 35/35 citation closure. Agreement spans two effective non-producer families, so it is materially stronger than head-count agreement within OpenAI alone.

The residual defects do not attack C1, AX-SH's four-axiom structure, or the scoped diagnostic layer. They are provenance and packet-boundary defects. The most important is line 19. The count of 44 is accurate, but the sentence collapses incidents from capability evaluations, public resources, and company disclosures into “the same assessment window” and “deployed frontier agents.” METR's own source breakdown does not support that compression ([METR](https://metr.org/blog/2026-05-19-frontier-risk-report/)). This is a surgical MEDIUM repair because it changes the empirical population described, not merely style.

The second MEDIUM issue is line 335's provenance disjunction. A packet-only auditor cannot verify inheritance from an absent “verified corpus bibliography,” and several entries do not carry inline notes. The content of those entries may still be correct, but chain provenance and content correctness are separate. The sentence should either be removed, limited to entries actually carrying packet-visible notes, or supported by an attached public corpus-bibliography receipt.

Two LOW issues remain. The bibliography comment says 36/36 despite deterministic closure at 35/35. Front-matter line 5 says monograph materials “appear” even though the packet cannot establish that present-tense fact. Neither affects the thesis, and both are one-line repairs.

The models' principal divergence came from audit granularity. Gemini 3.1 Pro verified the named repair directives and returned READY. GPT 5.5 and GPT 5.4 searched for residual surface-substrate mismatches around those repairs and returned READY-WITH-CONDITIONS. On the underlying manuscript, the GPT-family conditions are supported by direct packet and primary-source evidence. This is not independent-family convergence on each MEDIUM finding, but the adjudicator can preserve them as substantiated conditions without inflating their support.

There is also a process-level severity contradiction. Gemini labeled successful demotion and build controls HIGH/CRITICAL while marking them VERIFIED; both GPT seats labeled the corresponding successful checks LOW. Because the spread exceeds two bands, the dispatch requires project-lead escalation. The likely cause is semantic: control importance versus defect impact. This synthesis does not auto-resolve it.

The manuscript recommendation is READY-WITH-CONDITIONS. The conditions are narrow, reversible, and do not reopen the paper's architecture. On adverse defect substance, the round has zero CRITICAL and zero HIGH across both effective families. For the convergence ledger, however, the round should remain pending until the project lead records the severity-semantics adjudication. If the lead confirms that severity applies to adverse defect impact and normalizes Gemini's VERIFIED control labels, this becomes qualifying round one; otherwise the clock does not advance.
