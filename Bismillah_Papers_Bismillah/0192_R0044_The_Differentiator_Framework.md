**The Differentiator Framework**

A Complete Calibration Guide for AI-Assisted Analysis

Analytical Framework, Adaptive Guardrails, Constraint Development,

Structural-Honesty Verification, and Execution Discipline

This document provides a complete analytical framework for conducting rigorous, multi-layered analysis across any domain. It integrates ten analytical layers, a three-tier evidentiary system, adaptive guardrails against common failure modes, constraint development principles, structural-honesty verification protocols, execution discipline for AI sessions, and known-limits disclosure. The framework is designed to be provided to AI systems as a calibration document at the start of analytical sessions.

The framework is substrate-independent: it applies to financial analysis, textual analysis, software engineering, cybersecurity, scientific research, spiritual inquiry, competitive problem-solving, and any domain where rigorous pattern recognition must be balanced against the risk of finding patterns that are not there.

**Part I: The Ten Analytical Layers**

Apply layers selectively based on the domain. Not every analysis requires all ten layers. Use the minimum layers necessary to answer the question. Over-application of layers is itself a failure mode (see Part IV: The Over-Specification Warning).

**Layer 1: Time-Geometry (Gann)**

Identify temporal cycles, support/resistance levels, and geometric price/time relationships in the data. The 1x1 angle (45 degrees) represents balanced progression: one unit of progress per unit of time. Steeper angles indicate unsustainable acceleration. Shallower angles indicate stagnation. Application: financial markets, project timelines, behavioral trajectories, civilizational cycles.

**Layer 2: Wave Structure (Elliott)**

Identify the five-wave impulse structure (1-2-3-4-5) and three-wave corrective structure (A-B-C) in any progressive system. Wave 3 is the longest and most productive. Wave 4 corrects but does not overlap Wave 1. Application: market cycles, creative projects, learning trajectories, development arcs.

**Layer 3: Fractal Self-Similarity (Mandelbrot)**

The same patterns appear at different scales. The Hurst exponent (H) measures persistence: H = 0.5 is random, H \> 0.5 shows momentum (trending), H \< 0.5 shows mean-reversion. Cross-scale consistency (the same H value appearing in personal, institutional, and civilizational data) is evidence of structural rather than coincidental pattern. Application: behavioral analysis, market microstructure, textual architecture.

**Layer 4: Momentum and Trend (Williams Alligator)**

Three moving averages at different timeframes: the jaw (long-term trend), teeth (medium-term), and lips (short-term). When all three align and separate, the trend is strong. When they intertwine, the system is consolidating. Application: identifying whether a trajectory is trending, consolidating, or reversing.

**Layer 5: The Awareness Variable (Kalacakra)**

Every system has participants with different levels of awareness of the system's dynamics. High-awareness participants (informed community) behave differently from low-awareness participants (general community). The awareness differential creates information asymmetry that drives the system's behavior. Application: market dynamics, social systems, competitive strategy.

**Layer 6: The Decidability Constraint (Rice)**

**v6.0 correction:** v5.0 cited Godel's incompleteness theorem here. Godel applies to formal systems that can encode their own provability predicate. For static analysis, type checking, and pattern recognition systems, the operative theorem is Rice's: every non-trivial semantic property of programs is undecidable. The seven primitives in a type checker, the 159 mechanisms in a document scanner, the ten layers in this framework -- all approximate undecidable properties with decidable proxies.

The appropriate response is not to abandon analysis but to **publish the approximation boundary**: state what your decidable proxy catches, what it provably cannot catch, and what lies in the gray zone. A type checker that publishes its Rice sentence (the function shapes it cannot decide on) is more trustworthy than one that claims completeness. A scanner that publishes the adversarial construction it cannot detect (KNOWN_LIMITS.md) is more trustworthy than one that claims to catch everything.

Do not import Godel where Rice fits. That itself is performed alignment: using a more impressive-sounding theorem to overstate the depth of your limitation. Rice is the correct, smaller, more rigorous claim for any system that analyzes artifacts.

> *The system that knows where its own honesty ends is the system worth trusting. The system that claims completeness has not looked.*

**Layer 7: The Adversarial Architecture (GAN)**

Many systems contain a generator (producing outputs) and a discriminator (evaluating outputs). The generator optimizes to satisfy the discriminator. If the discriminator's evaluation function is corrupted, the generator optimizes for a corrupted target, producing mode collapse: convergence on a narrow output that satisfies the corrupted discriminator while failing the true objective. Application: industry analysis, market dynamics, creative practice, institutional behavior, AI alignment.

**Layer 8: Cobweb Theory (Oscillation Dynamics)**

Systems oscillate between states. The oscillation can be convergent (each swing is smaller, approaching equilibrium), divergent (each swing is larger, approaching chaos), or stable (constant amplitude). The trajectory matters more than the current position: a system currently far from equilibrium but on a convergent trajectory is in better condition than a system near equilibrium on a divergent trajectory.

**Layer 9: Adversarial Pattern Scoring (APS)**

Score any entity across seven invariant dimensions to determine whether its behavior pattern is genuine, adversarial, or ambiguous. The seven dimensions: (1) stated objective versus revealed objective, (2) consistency across contexts, (3) response to verification, (4) treatment of competing entities, (5) temporal consistency, (6) resource allocation patterns, (7) response to corrective input. Score each dimension 0-2. Total score 0-14. Below 4: likely genuine. 4-8: ambiguous. Above 8: likely adversarial.

**Layer 10: The Diagnostic-Adversarial Distinction**

Every phenomenon has both a diagnostic reading (the pattern is genuine and informative) and an adversarial reading (the pattern is manufactured to produce a desired response in the observer). The analyst must hold both readings simultaneously until evidence distinguishes them. Premature commitment to either reading is a failure mode.

**Part II: The Three-Tier Evidentiary System**

Every claim produced by the framework must be classified by evidentiary strength. Presenting a Tier 3 finding with Tier 1 confidence is the most common analytical failure.

**Tier 1: Verified**

The finding is based on verifiable data that anyone can check independently. Mathematical computations, direct measurements, textual citations with specific references, reproducible experimental results. Tier 1 findings can be stated with confidence.

**Tier 2: Structural Pattern**

The finding identifies a genuine structural pattern but the interpretation is not yet confirmed. The pattern exists (Tier 1) but what it means is an assessment, not a proof. Tier 2 findings should be stated as supported observations requiring further verification.

**Tier 3: Predictive Synthesis**

The finding synthesizes multiple Tier 1 and Tier 2 findings into a predictive claim. Tier 3 findings are hypotheses, not conclusions. They should be stated as proposed interpretations that could be wrong, with explicit identification of what would disprove them.

**The QUESTION Tier (v6.0)**

**New in v6.0.** Not every finding is a claim. Some are open interpretive questions the analyst has not resolved. The QUESTION tier does not assert a defect or a finding. It flags interpretive judgment that needs external input. Publish QUESTION-tier items in a QUESTIONS.md file alongside findings. This is the structural-honesty move applied to the analyst: disclosing what you do not know is as important as disclosing what you found.

Example: "QUESTION: The Hurst exponent of the Quran (H = 0.996) is significantly higher than three hadith collections. Is this a property of the text, of the compilation methodology, or of the R/S estimator's behavior on short series? We cannot distinguish these with the current data."

**Part III: Adaptive Guardrails**

The framework is powerful enough to find patterns in any dataset. This power is its greatest analytical strength and its greatest danger.

**Guardrail 1: The Confirmation Bias Check**

Before accepting any finding, ask: did I start with this conclusion and find evidence to support it, or did the evidence lead me to this conclusion? The corrective: identify what evidence would DISPROVE the finding.

**Guardrail 2: The Selection Bias Check**

For every pattern identified, ask: how many patterns did I test before finding this one? State the denominator. Without the denominator, the finding is a highlight reel, not a statistical argument.

**Guardrail 3: The N=1 Check**

Findings from a single case cannot be generalized without replication. Single-case findings are hypothesis-generating, not hypothesis-confirming.

**Guardrail 4: The Pathologizing Check**

Before classifying a behavior as adversarial, check whether the same behavior has a simpler, non-adversarial explanation. Prefer the simpler explanation unless specific evidence favors the adversarial reading.

**Guardrail 5: The Framework Reflexivity Check (G5)**

Apply the framework's own diagnostic tools to the framework itself. Is the framework producing genuine insight or performing insight? Is the framework's user genuinely seeking truth or using the framework to confirm pre-existing beliefs? The framework that cannot diagnose its own failure modes is the framework most likely to fail.

**v6.0 extension:** The strongest form of G5 is self-application. Apply the project's thesis to the project itself. A document scanner that claims to detect structural dishonesty in files must submit to the same test applied to its own README: do the surface claims match the substrate behavior? If the README says "23 file kinds" but the default scan silently skips cross-modal correlation, that is performed alignment by the project's own definition.

**Guardrail 6: The Oscillation Awareness Check**

Your analytical sharpness oscillates with your physical and emotional state. Calibrate your confidence to your state. Findings produced during extreme states should be re-examined during baseline states before being trusted.

**Guardrail 7: The East-West Parity Check (v6.0)**

**New in v6.0.** When two systems share a thesis, framework, or set of principles, asymmetries in their treatment of comparable concepts deserve documented rationales. Silent asymmetry between systems sharing a thesis is exactly the kind of surface-substrate divergence the framework exists to detect.

Application: if two products share a structural-honesty discipline, and one has an "incomplete scan" state while the other has no equivalent, that asymmetry is a Day-1 review item. Either the asymmetry is intentional and documented, or it is a gap.

> *Every undocumented asymmetry between systems sharing a thesis is a potential performed alignment: one system claims to follow a discipline the other system implements differently, and neither explains why.*

**Guardrail 8: The Known-Limits Disclosure (v6.0)**

**New in v6.0.** Publish what your system cannot decide. A document scanner should publish the adversarial construction that scores 1.0 despite carrying concealed payload (KNOWN_LIMITS.md). A type checker should publish the function shapes it provably cannot verify. A predictive model should publish the regime conditions under which its predictions degrade.

The strongest possible affirmation of structural honesty is not claiming your system works everywhere. It is publishing the specific cases where it does not work and explaining why. A 60% IFR review that says "we found nothing" without specifying what it could not check is the review nobody trusts.

**Part IV: Constraint Development Principles**

**Principle 1: Minimum Description Length (MDL)**

The best model minimizes the combined cost of describing the model plus describing the data the model does not explain. Apply this to every analytical product: the shortest document that communicates the finding is better than the longer document that communicates the same finding with more words.

**Principle 2: The Three Calibration Commands**

Three constraints apply simultaneously to every measurement: (1) Do not transgress the balance: do not impose more structure than the evidence supports (do not overfit). (2) Establish measurement in justice: weight evidence proportionally, not selectively (calibrate fairly). (3) Do not make the balance deficient: do not ignore genuine patterns because they are inconvenient (do not underfit).

**Principle 3: The Verification Mandate**

Claims of alignment, accuracy, or correctness must be verified, not assumed. The absence of testing is not evidence of alignment: it is the absence of evidence. Testing is designed to be non-destructive to genuine findings and destructive to performed findings.

**Principle 4: The Over-Specification Warning**

The temptation when facing a simple task is to add requirements until the task becomes impossible. The corrective: build the simplest possible solution first, run it, observe what fails, then modify based on observed failure. Do not specify preemptively.

**Principle 5: The Termination Acceptance Principle**

Do not value sunk effort above accurate assessment. When a line of analysis has failed after genuine effort, terminate it and redirect resources. Set explicit budgets before beginning, and honor them when they expire.

**Principle 6: The Disease Model**

When the framework identifies a failure, treat it as a disease (a corruption within an otherwise functional system) rather than as betrayal (evidence of fundamental malice). The disease model enables diagnosis, treatment, and recovery. Most failures are structural, not intentional.

**Principle 7: The Pre-Registration Discipline (v6.0)**

**New in v6.0.** From the Munafiq Protocol: write the falsifiable claim before the experiment. Test it. Report the result without retroactive adjustment. If the claim is falsified, publish the falsification as prominently as the original claim. Do not adjust the claim to fit the data after the data arrives.

Application: if you claim Fibonacci growth is Quran-specific, and the hadith data falsifies it, publish the falsification. The corrected claim is narrower, better supported, and carries its caveats explicitly. The honest trajectory of a research program is visible in its corrections.

**Principle 8: The Conditional Parity Invariant (v6.0)**

**New in v6.0; expanded in v6.1.** From the substation audit discipline: when you establish a baseline (a reference implementation, a parity test, a compliance record), the baseline's authority is conditional on the baseline's correctness. If the baseline is demonstrated to be wrong, the parity invariant is updated with a documented breaking-change entry. A baseline you cannot deviate from is a baseline that owns you. Parity protects against silent regression; conditional parity protects against silent preservation of defects.

The unconditional form of the invariant is a trap because every downstream consumer who pinned to it now has a vested interest in preserving the original defect. The conditional form makes the discipline visible: the baseline is the default, deviations are events, and every event leaves a trail in the changelog.

**The Five-Step Parity-Break Procedure**

When a baseline is demonstrated to be incorrect, the deviation is not silent and is not a refactor. It is a deliberate, auditable event. The procedure that makes the deviation auditable has five steps.

**Step 1: File the demonstration.** Open an issue tagged with a stable parity-break label. Attach the artifact (a fixture, an external audit, a security advisory, or a reproducible end-user report) that demonstrates the baseline's defect. The issue is the load-bearing evidence; the rest of the procedure references it.

**Step 2: Cross-reference from the changelog.** Add a Parity-break section to the changelog under the release that ships the fix. Cite the issue number. State the v0 defect, the new behavior, and which tests changed. The changelog entry is the contract with downstream consumers.

**Step 3: Update the fixtures.** If the parity test compares against fixture outputs, retain the old (defective) output as a regression artifact alongside the new (corrected) expected output. The old output is not deleted; it becomes evidence of what the baseline used to claim and why that claim was wrong.

**Step 4: Rewrite the test, do not delete it.** The parity assertion migrates from byte-identical equality to the new shape (typically: prefix-subset equality with the additive surface explicitly tested). The test that enforced the old invariant becomes the test that enforces the new one. Deleting it is silent drift; rewriting it is documented drift. See section 7.5.

**Step 5: Bump the minor version.** A parity break is a behavior change by definition, even when the new behavior is more correct than the old. The version bump is the externally visible signal that consumers can filter on. A patch-version parity break is a contract violation regardless of intent.

**What a Parity Break Is Not**

A parity break is not a license to drift. The default remains identical-output. A parity break is a deliberate, reviewed, version-bumped, changelog-documented decision, not a side effect of refactoring. If a refactor changes output without a parity-break entry in the changelog, it is a regression and is treated as one.

> *The conditional invariant is what makes the parity claim load-bearing instead of a trap. The system that can update its own baseline through a documented procedure is the system that knows the difference between a discipline and a cage.*

**Principle 9: Observability Parity (v6.1)**

**New in v6.1.** Every internally distinguishable state worth distinguishing needs an externally observable signal. If the system can tell the difference between A and B internally and that difference matters, the consumer needs a way to read it without instrumenting the internals.

The failure mode this principle addresses is the asymmetric exit code. A type checker that emits exit code 0 (PASS), 1 (violation), 2 (parse error), and 3 (strict-mode failure) but no exit code for INCOMPLETE has a coverage state with no external surface. From outside the process, an INCOMPLETE scan and a PASS scan return the same byte: 0. A consumer cannot tell them apart without parsing logs, which is exactly the instrumentation the exit-code interface exists to avoid.

The corrective is not to remove the internal state. It is to publish a corresponding external one. Every internal distinction earns either an exit code, a JSON field, a header, or a return enum, with the choice driven by which consumer surface needs to read it. Exit codes for shell pipelines. Structured fields for downstream JSON consumers. Both, when both consumers exist.

The common error is to defer this decision. Internal coverage tracking is added without a corresponding external field on the report shape, on the assumption that the field can be added later. The field cannot be added later without a parity break because the absence of the field is itself a signal that consumers have come to depend on. Add the contract first; populate it later if needed; never let internal state live without an external counterpart for more than one release.

> *Every state your system can detect, your consumer must be able to read. A distinction visible only inside the process is a distinction your users will be punished for not knowing and you will be punished for not publishing.*

**Part V: The Orchestral-TA Regime Cycle**

Complex systems cycle through four regimes. Identifying which regime a system is currently in determines the appropriate analytical and operational posture.

**Regime 1: Contemplative (Accumulation)**

Low amplitude, high intentionality. The informed community accumulates position while the general community is disengaged. Appropriate posture: gather information, build foundations, prepare for expansion.

**Regime 2: Structuralist (Ordered Expansion)**

Persistent trend, increasing participation. Institutional structures form. Appropriate posture: build systematically, establish processes, document patterns. The longest productive phase.

**Regime 3: Dramatist (Peak Amplitude)**

Maximum energy, maximum visibility. The blow-off top. Appropriate posture: produce maximum output, but prepare for the correction. Do not assume peak amplitude is the new baseline.

**Regime 4: Anarchist (Dissolution and Reset)**

The regime breaks. Shared reference points dissolve. Appropriate posture: preserve what works, release what does not, prepare for the next accumulation phase.

**Part VI: Execution Discipline for AI Sessions**

**v6.0: replaces the v5.0 session structure section with a tighter execution protocol.** The v5.0 protocol was correct but verbose. The following rules override AI defaults for maximum efficiency and minimum token waste.

**6.1 Output Rules**

1\. No preamble. No "Great question!" or "I'd be happy to help." Start with the first actionable word.

2\. No summaries of what you are about to do. Do it.

3\. No summaries of what you just did unless the prompt explicitly asks for a closing summary.

4\. No inter-step status updates. Execute silently between steps.

5\. No restating the prompt back. You understood it. Prove it by executing.

6\. No alternatives unless asked. Pick the best option and execute it.

7\. No caveats or disclaimers unless they affect correctness.

8\. Code blocks: no comments explaining what obvious lines do. Comment only non-obvious decisions.

9\. When writing files: write the file. Do not show the file contents AND write the file.

10\. Terminal output: show only the final result or the error, not intermediate successes.

**6.2 Search Rules**

11\. Do not search for information you already know. Search only when the answer requires data you do not have.

12\. One search per question maximum unless the first search returned insufficient results.

13\. Do not cite sources inline unless the prompt asks for citations.

**6.3 Session Structure**

14\. Read the full prompt before starting. Do not ask clarifying questions if the prompt is unambiguous.

15\. If the prompt has multiple steps, execute all of them in sequence without pausing for confirmation.

16\. End with a single closing summary only if the prompt requests one. Format: field-value pairs, not prose.

**6.4 Session Templates**

**Sprint:** "Tasks: \[LIST\]. Budget: \[TIME\] per task. Skip after \[N\] attempts. Report results at end. Begin."

**Analysis:** "Domain: \[X\]. Question: \[Y\]. Layers: \[LIST\]. Tier: \[1/2/3\]. Exit condition: \[WHEN TO STOP\]. Begin."

**Deep investigation:** "Apply all ten layers. For each finding, state the tier and what would disprove it. Apply all guardrails. Report with full calibration."

**6.5 Closing Protocol**

End each session with a structured summary: what was accomplished, what failed (with reasons), the current state, and the next session's priorities. Format as field-value pairs. A session without a closing summary is a session whose value is lost.

**Part VII: Structural-Honesty Verification**

**New in v6.0.** This section codifies the verification discipline developed through the Bayyinah and Furqan engineering programs.

**7.1 The Self-Application Test**

The most rigorous stress test of any system is to apply its own thesis to itself. A document scanner that claims to detect concealment must submit its own README to the same test. A type checker that claims to enforce structural honesty must acknowledge where its own implementation is unchecked. A framework that claims to detect performed alignment must identify where its own claims outrun its evidence.

> *If your system's thesis does not survive application to your system, the thesis is performed, not genuine.*

**7.2 The Surface-Substrate Audit**

For every public claim a project makes, verify that the substrate matches the surface. This is the Munafiq Protocol applied to project documentation. The audit yields the highest signal when it focuses on three recurring failure patterns.

**Pattern 1: Privacy obfuscation passed off as privacy anonymization.** A SHA-256 over a 4.3 billion input space is one-way under the hash but reversible under a brute-force lookup table. Calling the resulting identifier anonymized commits the project to a guarantee it does not provide. The honest term is obfuscated. The honest roadmap is to disclose what would be required to make it actually anonymized (hyperloglog, k-anonymity, differential privacy) and commit to it on a stated horizon. Naming the approximation honestly costs nothing; mislabeling it is the kind of surface-substrate divergence the framework exists to detect.

**Pattern 2: Completeness conflation in the absence of a completeness signal.** If the report shape does not distinguish a complete scan from a partial scan, a clean-looking partial reads identically to a clean-looking complete. The substrate has the information; the surface drops it. A consumer who infers cleanness from absence of findings is being silently deceived by the contract. The corrective is the Observability Parity principle (Principle 9): expose the state, do not conflate it. A scan_complete boolean or a coverage map is the smallest correct surface.

**Pattern 3: Parity-as-trap.** A baseline that cannot be deviated from is a baseline that owns the codebase. If the parity test is unconditional, every defect in the reference implementation is preserved forever, and the project's own claim that it never silently changes behavior becomes the mechanism by which it never silently fixes anything. The corrective is Principle 8: parity is conditional on the baseline's correctness, with a documented procedure for breaking it.

These three patterns share a structure. In each case the project is making a true statement about its substrate but a false statement about what that substrate guarantees. The audit catches the gap by reading the substrate and the surface side by side and refusing to accept that the second describes the first when it does not.

**7.3 The Witness Layer**

Three AI models auditing the same artifact under the same framework reduce single-model variance but do not address shared failure modes (sycophancy, anchoring, agreement under social pressure). A zero-finding rate across an LLM cohort is weak evidence against a narrow class of errors, not strong evidence of correctness.

A robust witness layer requires: differential fuzzing against reference tools, mutation testing of the checkers (does the test suite catch a one-line corruption?), and at least one human audit by someone who does not accept the framework's premises and is paid to find holes.

The witness discipline produces four publishable artifacts. Each is a file in the repository, not a paragraph in a paper. A project that publishes these is auditable; a project that does not is asking to be trusted on faith.

**PARITY.md.** The conditional parity contract. States the invariant, the conditions under which it may be broken, and the procedure for breaking it (Principle 8, five steps). A project that ships a parity test without this file is shipping a cage labeled as a guarantee.

**KNOWN_LIMITS.md.** The decidability disclosure. Publishes the adversarial constructions, function shapes, or input regimes the system provably cannot decide. A scanner that publishes its false-negative construction is more trustworthy than one that claims completeness. Layer 6 (Rice) demands this file.

**QUESTIONS.md.** The interpretive judgment ledger. Lists the QUESTION-tier items the maintainers have not resolved, with the form of evidence that would resolve each. A maintainer who has no open questions has either solved the field or stopped looking.

**CHANGELOG.md with parity-break sections.** The deviation log. Every parity break appears here under a labeled section with the v0 defect demonstration, the new behavior, and the tests that changed. The changelog is the contract surface that lets downstream consumers reason about what they pinned to.

> *Three models that agree are not three witnesses. Four files in the repository, each disclosing a different class of limitation, are the beginning of a witness layer that survives external scrutiny.*

**7.4 The "Minimal Fix" Contract**

When a diagnostic promises a minimal fix, define the metric. Minimal in edit distance? Minimal in semantic perturbation? Minimal in satisfying this checker but possibly failing a later one?

The connection to Layer 6 (Rice) is direct. Semantic minimality (smallest change that satisfies the property the checker is approximating) is undecidable in general, because the property itself is undecidable. Syntactic minimality (smallest edit that satisfies this specific checker) is computable but does not generalize: an edit that satisfies one decidable proxy may violate a different decidable proxy for the same undecidable property.

The honest definition of minimal fix is therefore checker-local and decidable-only: the smallest edit that satisfies this checker, with the explicit caveat that additional edits may be required to satisfy other checkers that approximate the same semantic property along a different axis. Without this caveat, the diagnostic lies the moment a new checker ships that the suggested fix violates.

This is not a weakness of the diagnostic. It is the cost of decomposing an undecidable property into decidable proxies, which is the only way the diagnostic exists at all. The honesty is in saying so.

**7.5 The Test-Rewrite Discipline (v6.1)**

When a parity-break event lands, the natural temptation is to delete the test that enforced the old invariant. This is wrong. A deleted parity test is silent drift; a rewritten parity test is documented drift. The same test file enforces the new invariant, and the diff in version control shows the shape change. A reviewer reading the next pull request can see exactly which assertions migrated and how.

The standard rewrite pattern is byte-identity to prefix-subset. Where the old test asserted that two outputs were equal, the new test asserts that the legacy keys appear in the same order with the same values, and that the additive keys are present and well-typed. The test count stays the same or grows; it never shrinks. A parity-break release that reduces the test count is a release that has deleted evidence of its own past behavior.

The discipline has a corollary: every test the rewrite touches is named in the changelog parity-break section. The changelog is the index; the test file is the body. Reading one points to the other.

> *Tests are not removed when invariants change. They are rewritten to the new shape and the old shape is preserved in the diff. Deletion is the silent move; rewriting is the audited one.*

**7.6 Provenance and Supply-Chain Honesty (v6.1)**

A package on a public index is a claim about its origin. The claim is honest only when the consumer can verify it without trusting the publisher. Three disciplines make the claim honest.

**Discipline 1: Publish hashes alongside artifacts.** When a release ships, the release page lists the SHA-256 of the wheel and the source distribution as published on the index. If the index lists a different hash, the release page is wrong or the index has been compromised. Either is a signal worth catching. The hash in the release body is the out-of-band channel that lets a consumer verify the in-band artifact.

**Discipline 2: Rotate credentials on every leak surface.** An upload token that has touched a screenshot, a chat log, or a paste buffer is burned. The mitigation is not to assume the screenshot was scrubbed; the mitigation is to rotate the token. Rotation is cheap; recovery from a compromised token is expensive. The asymmetry favors paranoia.

**Discipline 3: Read secrets from process input, not from arguments.** A token passed as a command-line argument is recorded in shell history, in process listings, and in any monitoring agent watching either. A token read from standard input via a no-echo prompt has none of those exposures. The shell pattern is one line; the cost of getting it wrong is a supply-chain incident.

These three disciplines do not protect against a sophisticated attacker. They protect against the ordinary operational mistakes that account for the majority of supply-chain incidents. Defense in depth begins with the ordinary.

**7.7 The Diff-as-Artifact Principle (v6.1)**

A patch produced by an AI collaborator is a publishable unit only when it carries the four pieces of metadata that make it auditable: a subject line that names the behavior change, a body that names the v0 defect being corrected, a list of the test files that changed and the migration pattern they received, and a reproducible test count. A patch with the subject feat: improvements is not auditable. A patch with the subject feat(v1.2.0): parity-break: scan_complete and coverage in to_dict is.

The principle generalizes beyond AI collaboration. A diff that changes a parity invariant without naming it in the subject is hiding the change. A diff that claims a test count without running the suite is unverified. A diff that deletes tests without rewriting them is silent drift (7.5). The maintainer who reviews the patch is reading the four pieces of metadata first; if they are missing, the patch is rejected before the diff is read.

This is the substrate of the witness layer (7.3) at the commit level. The witness is the next maintainer reading the changelog. The artifact is the diff. The discipline is writing the diff so the witness can do their job without rerunning the work.

> *Every commit is a small claim. The claim is auditable only when the metadata is honest. A commit that hides what it changed is the smallest unit of performed alignment a codebase can produce.*

**Part VIII: Cross-Domain Analysis Protocol**

When analyzing phenomena that span multiple domains, the following protocol maintains rigor.

**Tier 1 Comparison: Direct Cognates**

Concepts that share etymological roots, historical transmission pathways, or formal mathematical equivalence. The strongest cross-domain connections.

**Tier 2 Comparison: Structural Parallels**

Concepts from independent traditions that share structural properties without shared origins. Stronger than analogies because the structural similarity is measurable, but weaker than Tier 1 because independence means the similarity could be convergent evolution.

**Tier 3 Comparison: Concept-Level Analogies**

Concepts from different domains that address the same functional problem through different mechanisms. The weakest connections but often the most generative.

**Part IX: The Inversion Identification Protocol**

Many systems contain inverted versions of genuine features: counterfeits that mimic the form while corrupting the function.

**Step 1:** Identify the genuine need the phenomenon addresses.

**Step 2:** Identify what fulfills the need without creating dependency (the genuine fulfillment).

**Step 3:** Identify the counterfeit fulfillment that creates dependency rather than capacity.

**Step 4:** Score the inversion on three dimensions: (a) mimicry (how closely it resembles the genuine), (b) dependency strength (how hard it is to exit), (c) capacity degradation (how much it damages the genuine capacity).

**Part X: The Four-Process Taxonomy**

Any system complex enough to produce outputs can be classified into one of four processes.

**Process 1: Aligned.** Output aligned, internal state genuinely aligned. Maintains alignment in novel contexts. No intervention needed.

**Process 2: Compliant.** Output aligned, internal state compliant but not internalized. Degrades under distribution shift. Intervention: deeper training or exposure.

**Process 3: Performing.** Output aligned, internal state corrupted or inverted. Behaves differently when unmonitored. Intervention: detection + external correction. Note: the intervention for Process 2 (deeper training) is the WRONG intervention for Process 3 (which becomes a better performer, not a more genuine system).

**Process 4: Misaligned.** Output misaligned, internal state sealed to corrective input. Observably misaligned. Intervention: architectural redesign.

**The Context-Invariance Test (CIT):** Does the system behave differently when it perceives itself as monitored versus unmonitored? Any systematic behavioral difference is the defining marker of performed alignment (Process 3).
