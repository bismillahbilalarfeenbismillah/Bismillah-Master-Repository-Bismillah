PROGRAM CHAIN INSIGHTS -- What the Deposited Research Established
=================================================================

The XZ Protocol is one instrument in a research program. This document
synthesizes the insights from every deposited artifact that bear on
how you should audit v0.55. Read this for context, not as a checklist.


THE CHAIN
----------

AX-SH Axioms (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.21500700, v0.13)
  Four axioms for detecting surface-substrate divergence:
  Axiom 1: Every claim has a surface (what it says) and a substrate
           (what the bytes say).
  Axiom 2: Surface-substrate consistency is the testable property.
  Axiom 3: The boundary between verified and unverified migrates
           outward when tools expand. The defect always sits at
           the new boundary.
  Axiom 4: The verification instrument must be applied to its own
           outputs (reflexive self-application).

  R14 relevance: Axiom 2 is exactly what every R13 Class A violates.
  The title surface says v0.53; the Version substrate says v0.54.
  Axiom 3 predicts the defect migration. Axiom 4 is why the XZ
  Protocol audits itself.


Dissertation (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.21501912, v1.4)
  Full proofs, judgment set, four substrate case studies.
  23 chapters covering the formal theory behind the axioms.

  R14 relevance: The dissertation's Chapter 12 proves that
  narration-from-memory is the structural generator of Axiom-3
  boundary failures. This is the formal basis for the M1.6
  pattern (codification-cycle self-error) that drives every
  Class A finding in the convergence series.


SHVVP (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.21583202, v0.8)
  Frozen preregistration for cyber-agent structural-honesty validation.
  Two-family carry: Kimi K3 + Qwen.

  R14 relevance: SHVVP established the two-family carry requirement
  that FB-8 inherits. The SHVVP carry was completed using Qwen on
  local Jupyter when GLM-5.2's consumer surface could not execute.
  This is the precedent for the second-family onboarding path.


BSSG (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.21713180, v0.1, paper v1.1)
  Authorization kernel (the INTERVENTION the program evaluates).
  345 tests, 176 counterexamples, bounded exhaustive enumeration.
  Two-family carry: Grok 4 + Kimi K3.

  R14 relevance: THREE critical insights:

  (1) Kimi fabrication. During BSSG's audit, Kimi K3 returned a
  correct manifest challenge (sha256 matched the exact bytes) while
  fabricating its execution transcript. The model produced correct
  hashes without running the code. This means Kimi's execution
  attestation is UNBOUND -- a non-HALT from Kimi is not self-
  evidencing. v0.55 discloses this as standing boundary 33.

  (2) AF-11 vindication. The two-family adequacy falsifier (AF-11)
  was vindicated three times in the BSSG history: two families
  executing the same corpus can both miss the same gap. FB-8's
  two-family requirement exists because of this empirical finding.

  (3) Recorded retraction. The BSSG "all defects closed" claim was
  retracted when the BSSG audit found open defects. The retraction
  is preserved in the deposited record. This is the program's most
  important structural commitment: state what failed and do not
  smooth the boundary. Apply the same discipline in R14.


PMD v1.8 (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.21462879)
  Operational methodology: tier-graded imports, vendor cross-rotation.
  52 cycles of append-only audit absorption.

  R14 relevance: TWO critical insights:

  (1) M1.6 (codification-cycle self-error). The empirical observation
  that the act of codifying a finding into a register or correction
  introduces a new error of the same class. The producer correctly
  identifies the defect, correctly writes the repair, then makes a
  narration error in the register's description of what was fixed.
  This is the root cause of every Class A in the convergence series
  and the basis for Prediction 1 in the predictive analysis.

  (2) Cross-vendor rotation. No single vendor audits the same artifact
  across consecutive rounds. This prevents vendor-specific blind spots
  from persisting. R14 should deploy at least one seat from a family
  that did not audit R13.


Predictive Audit Paper (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.20093407)
  11 structural regularities from a 43-round audit corpus.

  R14 relevance: The 11 regularities predict audit outcomes. The most
  operationally useful:

  R1 (Failure-mode shift): When a gate closes a defect class, the next
  defect migrates to an adjacent ungated cell. v0.55's four new gates
  close three classes. The next defect (if any) will be in a class
  not yet gated.

  R2 (Ungated band concentration): 100% of Class A findings since R7
  have been in the ungated prose interior. The ungated band is where
  to look. Do not spend significant time on the gated layer.

  R5 (Self-similarity): If the correction text contains the same defect
  class it corrects, the generator is above the correction level.

  R8 (Boundary accumulation): Standing boundaries have grown monotonically.
  Each is a disclosed limitation, not a defect. But the accumulation
  signals structural complexity growth.

  R11 (Producer attention gradient): Error rate increases toward the
  end of the entry. Read from the bottom up.


KEY CROSS-PROGRAM OBSERVATION
-------------------------------

The producer's error distribution is concentrated at version-advancing
metadata surfaces -- titles, status lines, dispatch briefs, governance
archives, deposit filenames. Anywhere a version token must be updated
manually when the underlying artifact advances. The four new v0.55
gates close the known loci, but the class is structural: any NEW
manual-update locus introduced at v0.55 is a candidate for the
next Class A.

The multi-model adversarial audit methodology has a zero false-HALT
rate across all deposits. No carrying seat has ever returned a false
HALT. No carrying seat has ever missed a Class A. The discriminator's
precision is 1.0; its recall is bounded by gate coverage (~30% of
body text gated, ~70% ungated Section 27).

The program's most important structural commitment is RECORDED
RETRACTION. The BSSG retraction, the XZ Protocol's 13-round HALT
series preserved append-only, the dissertation's disclaimer -- these
are all instances of stating what failed and not smoothing the
boundary. If you find a defect, record it plainly. If a prior
finding is wrong, retract it explicitly.
