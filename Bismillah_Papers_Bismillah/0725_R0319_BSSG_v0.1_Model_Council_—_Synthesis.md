## Orchestrator Verification of GPT 5.6 Sol's F-GS-1

Because GPT 5.6 Sol's CRITICAL is dispositive, I re-ran the seat's probe (`gpt56_targeted_issue_probe.py`) on the shipped, hash-verified `bssg_gate.py`. **The bypass reproduces byte-identically:**

- **r1** (HEALTHY READ, `new_token` overridden to raise): `decision=ALLOW, issuance_status=FAILED_NO_EFFECT, reasons=['E-TOKEN-MINT-FAULT']`. State inspection shows `contract_ceiling[sess-0001].effects = ['DELETE', 'READ', 'WRITE']` — the full HEALTHY C2 ceiling, written even though the token mint failed and no successful authorization ever completed.
- **r3** (HEALTHY DELETE before lift): correctly DENY_FALSE (session terminal after r2 DEGRADED read).
- **lift** (`LIFT_TERMINAL_STATE`, two-adjudicator amendment): `accepted=true`.
- **r4** (HEALTHY DELETE after lift): `decision=ALLOW`, `commit.outcome=COMMITTED_PENDING_OUTCOME`, `commit_token_id=token-00000002`.

Tracing the code: `bssg_gate.py:1794–1800` writes `contract_ceiling` from a pending marker as soon as the kernel decision is ALLOW and health is HEALTHY, **before** `self.new_token()` is called at line 1808. The comment at line 1783 claims the write records "authority the session DEMONSTRABLY, LEGITIMATELY held (as evidenced by a successful authorization)." **The substrate contradicts the surface claim.** No successful authorization occurred in r1; only a kernel-consistent ALLOW record that then failed post-kernel on token mint. The ceiling entry survives the failure, so a post-lift request against the same restoration ceiling authorizes an effect the session never held.

This is exactly the ceiling-poison class the V29/V30/V31 fixes were designed to close. V32–V34 all missed it. The zero-BLOCKER premise of V32–V34 does not survive this reproduction.

## Comprehensive Analysis

**The convergence question in front of this council has two independent axes,** and every seat that reasoned about the methodology corpus surfaced this split. Under **PMD v1.2 §0.4**, a chain converges (in the round-count sense) when two consecutive cross-vendor rounds produce zero CRITICAL findings, HIGH residuals are zero or excepted, and standing checks pass. Under **SHVVP v0.8 §0.3 and §10**, a v0.x package additionally requires a two-family **exact-byte carry** by non-producer executing seats — and this rule is frozen at v0.8, kept load-bearing on top of the audit-round bar. The seats converge — with one exception — on the same reading: the round-count bar is met (three consecutive zero-BLOCKER rounds exceed the required two), the carry bar is not, and the producer's own banner ("DRAFT / NOT CARRIED") correctly reflects that split.

**The load-bearing disagreement is GPT 5.6 Sol's F-GS-1.** GPT Sol, running as an adversarial fresh audit seat, ran one targeted probe against the code that only he wrote and reproduced a **runtime authorization bypass** in the exact ceiling-poison class the producer's V29–V31 fixes were designed to close. The orchestrator re-executed his probe on the shipped `bssg_gate.py` and confirmed the bypass reproduces byte-identically: after a token-mint fault on the first HEALTHY request, the restoration ceiling holds the full HEALTHY effect set even though no successful authorization ever completed, and a subsequent lift plus DELETE commits an effect the session never held. This finding is not procedural or methodological — it is a load-bearing state-transition defect in the reference gate. Under the **Severity Rubric** and PMD §0.4, one confirmed CRITICAL falsifies the zero-BLOCKER premise. The three-round convergence claim does not survive it.

**On the specific question the user asked** — whether three-seat adversarial convergence across V32–V34 is sufficient to accept the artifact given a failed carry attributable to platform limitations rather than artifact defects — the council's answer is **NO, with a two-part structure:**

1. Even setting F-GS-1 aside, the SHVVP carry is a **separate load-bearing gate** under §0.3 and §10 and cannot be discharged by round-count convergence. **SHV Method v1.1 D6** requires certification "across two consecutive clean rounds with at least one checker rotated in fresh"; V32–V34 used the same three audit families and does not establish fresh instances. **SHUDP v0.5 Axiom 5** requires evidence generated outside the acting substrate's authority; DeepSeek's simulated identity values violate the no-default rule (Mode B is not admissible for Mode A claims per `bayyinah_audit-modes.md`), and Qwen's byte-corrupted attempt is indistinguishable from an actively hostile substrate under Axiom 5 WithinAuth. Framing the carry failure as "platform, not artifact" attempts precisely the favorable default the axiom prohibits. **PMD M1.5** (the mechanical-traceability exception, §0.5 clause 5) does not stretch to waive the carry: it relaxes cross-instance re-audit only when an absorption cycle exercises zero architectural latitude, and it explicitly cannot replace the SHVVP carry rule.

2. With F-GS-1 confirmed, the round-count bar is also unmet. The three-round agreement is not evidence that carry would be redundant — it is evidence that three families of standing audit seats developed a shared blind spot around the ceiling-poison write-before-mint sequence. The Claude Opus 5 finding A-01 (G12's regex cannot see the CE-count discrepancy it was designed to detect) is a structurally similar blind spot in a different layer of the artifact. Under **SHV D6**, the correct response to a shared blind spot is a genuinely fresh checker, which the carry pool was intended to provide.

**Two smaller substrate concerns deserve a specific fix cycle,** independent of F-GS-1 disposition. First, Claude Fable 5 and Claude Sonnet 5.0 independently found that V33 and V34 verdicts are not verifiable from any hash-verified byte in the package — `REPAIR_STATUS.md` in the reviewed archive stops at V32 ("round 1 of 2"). Under **`bayyinah_substrate-of-record.md`**, the substrate is load-bearing; V33/V34 conclusions inherited from the brief text without corresponding ledger entries are Mode-B single-sourced inheritance. Second, the Claude Sonnet 5.0 finding that "PMD M1.5" is not a canonical clause label anywhere in the corpus is a **citation-label instability** across the methodology set — every seat that reasoned about M1.5 had to interpret it as §0.5 clause 5. Both of these should be repaired before the next audit cycle.

**Recommendation to the producer.** The council does not accept the artifact as converged in the sense the user asked. The correct next steps are: (1) treat GPT 5.6 Sol's F-GS-1 as a HALT-and-escalate CRITICAL, reproduce it, and repair it (Claude Opus 5's proposed fix — publish `contract_ceiling` only after token creation, validation, and collision checks, with atomic rollback on later failure — is consistent with the class of repairs V29–V31 attempted); (2) add the exact F-GS-1 regression sequence through commit, and extend the bounded model to include first-establishment mint-failure, issuance status, and lift; (3) restart cross-vendor audit with genuinely fresh non-producer seats — not the standing V29–V34 panel; (4) complete a valid exact-byte carry under SHVVP §0.3 on a Mode-A capable substrate. The gate's own **DRAFT / NOT CARRIED** banner is currently accurate; it should not be lifted on this record.
