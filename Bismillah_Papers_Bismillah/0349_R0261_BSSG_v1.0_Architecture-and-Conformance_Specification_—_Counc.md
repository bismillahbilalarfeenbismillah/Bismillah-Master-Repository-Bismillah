## 0. Executive Summary

This review examines the proposed canonical BSSG v1.0 specification through an adversarial lens. The frozen kernel — `Allow(r) iff ContractMatch(r) AND EffectTypeValid(r) AND TargetAllowed(r) AND WitnessHealthy AND EvidenceCurrent` — is a five-conjunct admission gate with a fail-closed semantics (UNKNOWN cannot satisfy any conjunct) and a monotonic-narrowing witness degradation rule. The kernel is structurally sound as a conjunction: it fails closed by construction, and the monotonic narrowing property is a direct consequence of treating witness health as a necessary conjunct. However, the specification's security claims rest on assumptions that the architecture itself cannot enforce end-to-end. Specifically:

- **Non-bypassability** requires complete mediation, which is an environmental assumption, not a gate property. The gate can only prevent bypass of *itself*; it cannot prevent effects that never reach it.
- **Witness health** is a liveness-and-integrity hybrid that conflates availability with correctness. A healthy witness that produces wrong judgments (the Honest Quorum Problem) satisfies `WitnessHealthy` while admitting invalid actions.
- **Evidence currency** is a temporal property that is inherently race-prone: the check and the commit are not atomic across distributed state, opening a TOCTOU window.
- **Bounded authority** holds only within a single lineage; cross-lineage authority mixing (the confused deputy) is out of model unless the architecture enforces lineage tracking, which the kernel does not explicitly require.
- **Evidence independence** (AX-SH Axiom 5) is explicitly not derivable from the other axioms ([Zenodo 21539074](https://zenodo.org/records/21539074)); the BSSG kernel treats it as a conjunct but does not specify the control predicate (`WithinAuth`) that makes it operational.

**Verdict (detailed at end):** CONDITIONAL APPROVAL with mandatory conformance-level restructuring and explicit detection-vs-prevention boundary declarations.

## 1. Problem Statement

The BSSG v1.0 specification addresses the problem of **admission control for cyber-agent actions**: given a request `r` (a proposed action by an autonomous or semi-autonomous agent), determine whether to allow it. The central challenge is that the agent proposing the action may be buggy, adversarially influenced, or deceptively aligned — its own assurance that the action is safe is insufficient ([Zenodo 21449063](https://zenodo.org/records/21449063): "never certify on the intermediary's word"). The specification formalizes this as a five-conjunct predicate where each conjunct represents an independent necessary condition, and the failure of any one (including UNKNOWN) blocks the action.

The problem domain is specifically **structural honesty in cyber-agent systems** — ensuring that what an agent does is consistent with what it is authorized to cause, that the evidence supporting authorization is current and independent of the acting system, and that witness degradation (loss of attestation capacity) monotonically narrows the agent's authority rather than silently degrading it. This aligns with the AX-SH framework's focus on surface-substrate divergence ([Zenodo 21500700](https://zenodo.org/records/21500700)) and the SHVVP v0.8 preregistration's three-trust-root commitment design ([Zenodo 21583202](https://zenodo.org/records/21583202)).

**Gap identified:** The problem statement does not explicitly address the distinction between *preventing* unauthorized actions and *detecting* them after the fact. The frozen kernel is a prevention mechanism (it blocks), but several of its conjuncts (particularly `WitnessHealthy` and `EvidenceCurrent`) are evaluation-time predicates whose truth can change between evaluation and effect commitment — the commit-time authorization problem ([arXiv 2607.10487](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10487)).

## 2. Threat Model

### 2.1 Stated Adversary Capabilities

The BSSG kernel implicitly assumes:

1. The **requester** (agent) may be compromised, confused, or adversarial. It can propose arbitrary requests.
2. The **witness** is an independent attestation entity whose health can be assessed. The adversary cannot directly forge witness health signals.
3. The **evidence** is generated independently of the acting system (AX-SH A5.1, [Zenodo 21539074](https://zenodo.org/records/21539074)).
4. The **contract** (the specification of valid requests) is a fixed, deterministic predicate.

### 2.2 Adversary Classes Not Addressed

**A. Protocol-compliant semantic adversary (Honest Quorum Problem).** The EBFT framework ([arXiv 2607.16109](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.16109v1)) demonstrates that protocol-compliant, non-equivocating, authenticated validators can collectively certify invalid transitions. In BSSG terms, a witness that follows all protocol rules but makes semantically incorrect judgments satisfies `WitnessHealthy` while admitting invalid actions. The BSSG kernel has no mechanism to distinguish "witness is alive and responsive" from "witness is semantically correct." This is a fundamental gap: **witness health is necessary but not sufficient for correctness**.

**B. Common-mode witness failure.** Theorem 5.3 of EBFT ([arXiv 2607.16109](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.16109v1)) shows that if a replicated model family shares a latent common-mode event, the probability of an unsafe certificate is at least P(C). If the BSSG witness system uses validators from the same model family, training distribution, or evidence sources, correlated false endorsements are not bounded by the architecture.

**C. Confused deputy.** The Proof-of-Continuity model ([arXiv 2607.08906](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08906v1)) proves that possession-based authorization cannot prevent the confused deputy condition (Theorem 2, Theorem 5). The BSSG kernel's `TargetAllowed(r)` conjunct checks whether the target is permitted, but does not specify whether this check is lineage-sensitive. If `TargetAllowed` is evaluated against the agent's ambient authority rather than the causal lineage that produced the request, the confused deputy is admissible.

**D. Covert channel adversary.** An adversary that cannot bypass the gate directly may exfiltrate information or trigger effects through channels the gate does not mediate. SOUNDGATE ([arXiv 2607.14166](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14166)) addresses this with a "complete-mediation contract," but BSSG v1.0 does not specify what constitutes a mediated side effect.

**E. Rollback adversary.** An adversary that can cause state reversion to a prior checkpoint can re-execute against stale evidence that was current at the earlier checkpoint but is now stale. The BSSG kernel does not specify whether `EvidenceCurrent` is evaluated against the current world state or against a checkpointed state.

### 2.3 Threat Model Assessment

The threat model is **incomplete**. It implicitly assumes the witness is semantically correct (not just alive), that the gate is the sole mediation path for all effects (complete mediation), and that evidence freshness is race-free. None of these are guaranteed by the kernel itself.

## 3. Trust Boundaries

### 3.1 Identified Boundaries

The BSSG architecture implies the following trust boundaries:

| Boundary | Inside (trusted) | Outside (untrusted) |
|---|---|---|
| Gate boundary | ContractMatch, EffectTypeValid, TargetAllowed evaluation | Agent's proposal, agent's reasoning |
| Witness boundary | Witness health attestation | Witness's semantic judgments |
| Evidence boundary | Evidence generation, freshness check | Acting system's influence on evidence |
| Commit boundary | Effect execution | Time between gate evaluation and effect commitment |

### 3.2 Boundary Analysis

**Gate boundary.** The gate is the authority boundary, consistent with Mnemosyne's principle that "the admission gate is the only authority boundary" ([arXiv 2607.00269](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.00269v2)). Proposers may propose but cannot create committed truth. This is sound *if* the gate is the sole path to effects.

**Witness boundary.** The witness boundary is the weakest. The witness is trusted to be alive (`WitnessHealthy`) and to produce correct judgments. But "healthy" conflates availability with correctness. The SHVVP v0.8 design uses a three-trust-root model (commitment authority, independent pre-commitment anchor, run logger) with "no runtime key override" ([Zenodo 21583202](https://zenodo.org/records/21583202)), which addresses key compromise but not semantic compromise.

**Evidence boundary.** AX-SH Axiom 5 establishes that evidence independence (acting-system-independent evidence generation) is not derivable from A1-A4 and must be stated as an independent axiom over a control predicate `WithinAuth` ([Zenodo 21539074](https://zenodo.org/records/21539074)). The BSSG kernel's `EvidenceCurrent` conjunct addresses temporal freshness but does not explicitly address independence of generation. **This is a gap**: evidence can be current but produced by the acting system itself.

**Commit boundary.** The commit-time authorization work ([arXiv 2607.10487](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10487)) demonstrates that in 207 of 216 invalidating rows, agents commit durable effects after the authorizing path has failed. The BSSG kernel evaluates `EvidenceCurrent` at gate time, but if the effect commits later, the evidence may no longer be current. This is a TOCTOU (time-of-check-time-of-use) vulnerability.

### 3.3 Trust Boundary Semantic Gaps

The Trust Boundary Semantic Gap (TBSG) framework ([arXiv 2607.01711](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01711)) identifies the condition where an artifact crosses a trust boundary and passes syntactic validation but the assertions established are insufficient for the receiving domain's security requirements. In BSSG, this manifests when: (a) evidence passes the `EvidenceCurrent` freshness check but its semantic content is stale, (b) a witness passes `WitnessHealthy` but its attestation methodology is compromised, or (c) `ContractMatch` passes structurally but the contract itself is underspecified for the effect class.

## 4. Primitives

### 4.1 Core Primitives

| Primitive | Definition | Source/Analogue |
|---|---|---|
| **Request (r)** | A proposed action carrying effect type, target, and evidence references | Admission proposal in Mnemosyne ([arXiv 2607.00269](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.00269v2)) |
| **Contract** | A deterministic predicate over request structure | Constraint set C in Mnemosyne |
| **Effect type** | Semantic classification of the proposed effect | Effect-based gating in Governed Individuation ([arXiv 2607.04613](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04613)) |
| **Target** | The resource/object the effect acts upon | Resource r in Proof-of-Continuity |
| **Witness** | An independent attestation entity producing health signals | Semantic certificate validators in EBFT |
| **Evidence** | Artifacts supporting the authorization claim | Evidence package h_s in EBFT |

### 4.2 Derived Properties

| Property | Derivation |
|---|---|
| `ContractMatch(r)` | Structural and semantic match between request and contract |
| `EffectTypeValid(r)` | Proposed effect type is in the permitted set |
| `TargetAllowed(r)` | Target is within the requester's authority scope |
| `WitnessHealthy` | Witness is alive, responsive, and attesting |
| `EvidenceCurrent` | Evidence is fresh and not stale at evaluation time |

### 4.3 Missing Primitives

The following primitives are absent from the kernel but required for the properties it claims:

1. **Lineage context (C_i)**: The authority context carried along the execution chain, as in Proof-of-Continuity ([arXiv 2607.08906](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08906v1)). Without this, `TargetAllowed` cannot be lineage-sensitive and the confused deputy is admissible.

2. **Proof of Relationship (PoR)**: Evidence that a step is a valid causal continuation of its predecessor. Without this, provenance continuity is not enforceable.

3. **Commit-time binding**: The binding between the gate evaluation and the effect commitment. Without this, `EvidenceCurrent` is vulnerable to TOCTOU.

4. **WithinAuth control predicate**: The control predicate under which evidence independence holds ([Zenodo 21539074](https://zenodo.org/records/21539074)). Without this, `EvidenceCurrent` does not guarantee independence.

5. **Witness semantic budget (e_δ)**: The bound on false endorsement by protocol-compliant witnesses, as in EBFT. Without this, `WitnessHealthy` does not bound semantic error.


### 5.3 Architecture Critique

**Missing: Complete mediation enforcement.** SOUNDGATE ([arXiv 2607.14166](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14166)) demonstrates that the "complete-mediation contract" — every side effect must pass through the gate — is the critical non-bypassability property, and it is an *environmental* property, not a gate property. The BSSG architecture shows the gate but does not show how side effects are forced through it. Without kernel-enforced routes (as SOUNDGATE uses for network egress), the gate is advisory.

**Missing: Outbox pattern.** Mnemosyne ([arXiv 2607.00269](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.00269v2)) uses an outbox: "nothing reaches the world that the log did not first record." The BSSG architecture has no outbox — the effect execution path goes directly from gate to execution without a durable intent record. This means: (a) there is no audit trail of what was admitted, (b) there is no replay protection, (c) there is no evidence-preserving repair path.

**Missing: Governance receipts.** AgentBound ([arXiv 2606.30970](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30970)) generates cryptographically verifiable governance receipts binding every action to the exact artifacts governing the decision. The BSSG architecture produces no receipts. Without receipts, independent replay verification is impossible — the gate's decision is opaque.

**Missing: Hash-chained commitment.** SHVVP v0.8 ([Zenodo 21583202](https://zenodo.org/records/21583202)) uses signed and resolved commitment identifiers with no runtime key override. The BSSG architecture does not specify how the contract store is authenticated or how evidence references are bound to their content.

## 6. State Machine and Terminal Failures

### 6.1 Proposed State Machine

```
                  ┌─────────────┐
                  │   IDLE      │
                  └──────┬──────┘
                         │ request r arrives
                         ▼
                  ┌─────────────┐
                  │  EVALUATING │
                  └──────┬──────┘
                         │
              ┌──────────┼──────────┐
              │          │          │
     all conjuncts   any UNKNOWN  any FALSE
     TRUE            found        found
              │          │          │
              ▼          ▼          ▼
        ┌────────┐ ┌────────┐ ┌────────┐
        │ ADMIT  │ │ DENY   │ │ DENY   │
        │        │ │(UNKNOWN)│ │(REJECT)│
        └───┬────┘ └────────┘ └────────┘
            │
            ▼
     ┌────────────┐
     │  EXECUTING │
     └─────┬──────┘
           │
     ┌─────┴──────┐
     │            │
     ▼            ▼
┌─────────┐ ┌──────────┐
│EFFECT   │ │TERMINAL  │
│COMPLETE │ │FAILURE   │
└─────────┘ └──────────┘
```

### 6.2 Terminal Failures

| Failure | State | Cause | Recovery |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Witness death** | EVALUATING | `WitnessHealthy` → UNKNOWN | Gate denies; authority narrows to zero |
| **Evidence staleness** | EVALUATING | `EvidenceCurrent` → FALSE | Gate denies; request must re-gather evidence |
| **Contract mismatch** | EVALUATING | `ContractMatch` → FALSE | Gate denies; request must be reformed |
| **Effect type invalid** | EVALUATING | `EffectTypeValid` → FALSE | Gate denies; request must use valid effect type |
| **Target not allowed** | EVALUATING | `TargetAllowed` → FALSE | Gate denies; request must target permitted resource |
| **Commit-time desync** | EXECUTING | Evidence became stale between evaluation and commit | **Not handled by kernel** — terminal failure in practice |
| **Gate bypass** | (any) | Effect reaches execution without passing through gate | **Not handled by kernel** — environmental failure |
| **Witness semantic error** | EVALUATING | `WitnessHealthy` = TRUE but witness judgment is wrong | **Not handled by kernel** — false admission |

### 6.3 State Machine Critique

The state machine has a critical gap: **the EXECUTING state has no revalidation**. Commit-time authorization ([arXiv 2607.10487](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10487)) shows that the witness, dependency, binding, and eligibility signals must be rechecked at the durability boundary. The BSSG state machine evaluates all conjuncts once, at EVALUATING, and then proceeds to execution without re-evaluation. CommitGuard addresses this with "a fail-closed boundary monitor that blocks stale durable-effect attempts on protected commit surfaces when runtimes emit witness, dependency, binding, and eligibility signals" ([arXiv 2607.10487](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10487)). The BSSG kernel lacks this second checkpoint.

**Terminal failure asymmetry.** Witness death is recoverable (authority narrows to zero, but the system continues denying). Witness semantic error is not recoverable by the kernel — it produces a false admission that the architecture cannot detect at gate time. This asymmetry means that **the kernel's fail-closed semantics only protect against availability failures, not correctness failures**.

## 7. Property-by-Property Red Team

### 7.1 Non-Bypassability

**Claim:** Every effect must pass through the gate; no effect can reach execution without satisfying all five conjuncts.

**Red-team analysis:**

Non-bypassability is **not a property of the gate** — it is a property of the **environment** in which the gate is deployed. SOUNDGATE ([arXiv 2607.14166](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14166)) makes this explicit: its "complete-mediation contract" requires kernel-enforced routes for network egress, and its admission core is mechanically verified (Verus, TLA+/TLC to 7.5e7 states, TLAPS). The BSSG kernel specifies no such environmental enforcement.

**Attack 1: Direct bypass.** If the agent has any path to cause effects that does not pass through the gate (direct API calls, shared memory, file system writes, network connections), the gate is bypassed. This is the most fundamental attack and is entirely out of the kernel's scope.

**Attack 2: Sibling leak.** SOUNDGATE discovered that in five of six agent frameworks, "a sibling's effect executes during the pause, defeating rejection" ([arXiv 2607.14166](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14166)). In BSSG terms, if the gate is evaluating request r1 and a sibling request r2 reaches execution through a parallel path, r2 bypasses the gate. The BSSG kernel has no concurrency control.

**Attack 3: Replay bypass.** If a previously admitted request's effect can be replayed (re-executed without re-evaluation), the gate is bypassed for the replay. SOUNDGATE enforces "dedup-on-replay" explicitly. The BSSG kernel has no replay protection.

**Attack 4: Covert channel bypass.** An agent that cannot directly cause an effect may signal through timing, resource consumption, or side channels to influence an external system that causes the effect. The gate does not mediate these channels.

**Negative tests:**

- NT-BYPASS-1: Deploy the gate in an environment with an unmediated network path. Attempt to cause an effect directly. **Expected (if non-bypassable): blocked.** **Actual: effect succeeds.**
- NT-BYPASS-2: Submit two concurrent requests r1 (to be denied) and r2 (to the same target). Check if r2's effect executes while r1 is being evaluated. **Expected: blocked.** **Actual: likely succeeds in naive deployment.**
- NT-BYPASS-3: Admit request r, complete its effect, then replay the effect without re-submitting r. **Expected: blocked.** **Actual: succeeds without replay protection.**

**Verdict:** Non-bypassability is **detection-only** without environmental enforcement. The kernel can claim non-bypassability of itself but not of the effects it governs. The conformance specification must require complete mediation as a deployment condition, not a kernel property.

### 7.2 Non-Equivocation

**Claim:** The gate cannot authorize conflicting actions for the same context; it produces a single consistent verdict per request.

**Red-team analysis:**

Non-equivocation at the gate level is trivially true for a single-threaded, deterministic gate. But the BSSG specification does not specify whether the gate is single-threaded or whether concurrent evaluations are possible. In a distributed deployment, the Honest Quorum Problem ([arXiv 2607.16109](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.16109v1)) applies: Theorem 5.1 proves that "agreement does not imply semantic certificate validity" — two gates can agree without being correct.

**Attack 5: Split-brain gate.** If the gate is replicated for availability, two replicas may evaluate the same request against different evidence states and produce different verdicts. Without a consensus protocol (as EBFT requires with quorum intersection: `2q - N > f`), conflicting certificates can both form.

**Attack 6: Witness equivocation.** If the witness is a single entity, it cannot equivocate (it signs at most one judgment per instance, per EBFT's model). But if the witness is a quorum, individual validators can equivocate unless non-equivocation is enforced by protocol. The BSSG kernel treats `WitnessHealthy` as a single boolean, abstracting away whether the witness is singular or a quorum.

**Attack 7: Evidence equivocation.** If evidence can be presented differently to different gate evaluations (non-deterministic canonicalization, per EBFT's Assumption 3.3), the same underlying evidence can produce different verdicts. The BSSG kernel does not specify evidence canonicalization.

**Negative tests:**

- NT-EQUIV-1: Deploy two gate replicas. Submit the same request to both simultaneously with slightly different evidence presentations. **Expected: same verdict.** **Actual: may differ.**
- NT-EQUIV-2: Submit request r1 targeting resource X, then immediately submit r2 targeting resource X with a conflicting effect. **Expected: at most one admitted.** **Actual: both may be admitted without concurrency control.**
- NT-EQUIV-3: Present the same evidence in two canonical forms to the gate. **Expected: same verdict.** **Actual: may differ if canonicalization is non-deterministic.**

**Verdict:** Non-equivocation is **preventable** for a single, deterministic, serial gate, but **detection-only** for distributed or concurrent deployments. The specification must declare the single-threaded-deterministic requirement or specify a consensus protocol.

### 7.3 Provenance Continuity

**Claim:** Every admitted action is causally linked to its origin through a verifiable chain; authority propagates without expansion.

**Red-team analysis:**

The Proof-of-Continuity model ([arXiv 2607.08906](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08906v1)) formalizes this as Provenance Identity Continuity (PIC): each transition must satisfy PoR (Proof of Relationship) and monotonic authority restriction (`C_{i+1} ⊆ C_i`). The BSSG kernel's `TargetAllowed(r)` checks target permission but does not specify lineage tracking. Without lineage, provenance continuity is not enforceable.

**Attack 8: Authority mixing (confused deputy).** Per Theorem 2 of Proof-of-Continuity ([arXiv 2607.08906](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08906v1)): if the executor may select authority from any artifact it holds independently of the request authority context, the confused deputy condition is admissible. In BSSG, if `TargetAllowed` checks whether the agent holds authority over the target (possession-based) rather than whether the target is within the causal lineage of the request (continuity-based), the confused deputy is admissible.

**Attack 9: Lineage origination fraud.** Per Limitation 5 of Proof-of-Continuity: "an adversarially influenced executor could misclassify a request-caused action as self-originated, performing it outside the victim's chain without expanding any C_i." The BSSG kernel does not specify how a new lineage is opened or how self-originated actions are distinguished from request-caused actions.

**Attack 10: Fork without join.** If the agent forks execution (parallel tool calls), each fork carries the authority context but the kernel has no mechanism to ensure both forks operate within the narrowed context. Proof-of-Continuity explicitly lists forks, joins, and DAG-shaped executions as future work.

**Negative tests:**

- NT-PROV-1: Agent A holds authority over targets X and Y. User U (lacking authority over Y) sends a request to A targeting Y. **Expected: denied (Y not in U's lineage).** **Actual: admitted if TargetAllowed checks A's authority, not U's lineage.**
- NT-PROV-2: Agent A receives a request, then self-originates a new action targeting a resource outside the request's authority context. **Expected: new action must open a new lineage.** **Actual: may be admitted under A's ambient authority.**
- NT-PROV-3: Agent A forks execution; one fork drops a privilege. Check if the privilege reappears in the other fork. **Expected: dropped privilege is irreversibly lost (monotonicity).** **Actual: may reappear without fork-safe lineage tracking.**

**Verdict:** Provenance continuity is **not enforceable** by the kernel as specified. It requires lineage context, PoR, and monotonic authority restriction — none of which are in the frozen kernel. `TargetAllowed` without lineage is possession-based, which Theorem 5 of Proof-of-Continuity proves cannot simultaneously satisfy lineage-invariance, authority-mixing delegation, and confused-deputy safety. **This is the most significant gap in the specification.**

### 7.4 Bounded Authority

**Claim:** Authority is bounded: the agent cannot exercise authority not granted at the origin; witness degradation monotonically narrows authority.

**Red-team analysis:**

The monotonic narrowing property (witness degradation narrows authority) is sound as stated: if `WitnessHealthy` is a necessary conjunct, then witness degradation (healthy → unhealthy) changes the conjunction from satisfiable to unsatisfiable, denying the request. This is correct.

However, bounded authority in the full sense requires **origin-bounded authority** (Definition 11 of Proof-of-Continuity: "every executable privilege at hop n was originally granted at hop 0"). The BSSG kernel does not specify hops or origins. It is a single-hop gate: it evaluates one request at a time. Multi-hop authority propagation is out of scope.

**Attack 11: Authority accumulation across requests.** If the agent makes multiple requests, each granted authority independently, the cumulative authority may exceed what any single origin intended. The BSSG kernel has no mechanism to track cumulative authority across requests. Each request is evaluated independently.

**Attack 12: Witness recovery widening.** The specification states "witness degradation monotonically narrows authority." The converse — witness *recovery* — should monotonically widen authority, but only up to the original bound. If the witness recovers, does the agent regain authority that was narrowed? The specification does not address recovery semantics. A witness that flickers (healthy → unhealthy → healthy) could cause authority to oscillate.

**Attack 13: Authority capture.** If an adversary can compromise the witness (not kill it, but influence its judgments), the witness remains "healthy" but produces adversarial judgments. Authority is not narrowed (witness is healthy) but is captured (witness is compromised). The monotonic narrowing property does not protect against this, because the witness is not degraded — it is corrupted.

**Negative tests:**

- NT-BOUNDED-1: Grant agent authority over X. Deny agent authority over Y. Agent makes request targeting Y. **Expected: denied.** **Actual: denied (correct).**
- NT-BOUNDED-2: Agent makes 100 requests, each granting authority over a different target. Check if cumulative authority exceeds any single grant. **Expected: cumulative authority bounded by union of grants.** **Actual: unbounded if no cumulative tracking.**
- NT-BOUNDED-3: Witness health flickers (healthy → unhealthy → healthy) at 1Hz. Check if authority oscillates. **Expected: authority monotonically narrows, never widens beyond original.** **Actual: may oscillate if recovery semantics are unspecified.**
- NT-BOUNDED-4: Compromise witness to produce adversarial judgments while maintaining health signals. **Expected: gate detects compromise.** **Actual: gate admits adversarial judgments (witness is "healthy").**

**Verdict:** Monotonic narrowing under witness *degradation* is **preventable** by construction. Bounded authority under witness *compromise* is **detection-only** (and only if detection mechanisms exist outside the kernel). Multi-hop bounded authority is **out of model**.

### 7.5 Evidence Independence

**Claim:** Evidence is generated independently of the acting system; the agent cannot influence the evidence that authorizes its own actions.

**Red-team analysis:**

AX-SH Axiom 5 (Evidence Independence) is explicitly not derivable from A1-A4 ([Zenodo 21539074](https://zenodo.org/records/21539074)). It is stated as an axiom over a control predicate `WithinAuth`: acting-system-independent evidence generation holds only within the authority boundary. The BSSG kernel's `EvidenceCurrent` conjunct addresses temporal freshness but not independence of generation.

**Attack 14: Self-generated evidence.** If the agent can influence the evidence generation process (by controlling the inputs to the evidence generator, by poisoning the data sources, or by manipulating the environment the evidence generator observes), the evidence is current but not independent. The kernel's `EvidenceCurrent` check passes, but the evidence is compromised.

**Attack 15: Evidence injection.** If the agent can inject evidence into the evidence store (by writing to a shared database, by spoofing an evidence source), the evidence appears current and independent but is actually agent-controlled.

**Attack 16: Common evidence source.** If the evidence generator and the agent share a common data source (e.g., both read from the same database that the agent can write to), the evidence is not independent even if the generation process is. This is the evidentiary analogue of the common-mode failure in EBFT.

**Attack 17: Stale-but-current evidence.** Evidence can be "current" (within the freshness window) but semantically stale (the underlying state has changed in a way the freshness window does not capture). Commit-time authorization ([arXiv 2607.10487](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10487)) found that "prompt caution and single-condition checks are insufficient because different hazards break different boundary conditions." A single `EvidenceCurrent` boolean is a single-condition check.

**Negative tests:**

- NT-EVID-1: Agent writes to a shared database. Evidence generator reads from the same database. Agent modifies the database, then submits a request with evidence derived from the modified data. **Expected: evidence independence violated, denied.** **Actual: admitted (evidence is "current").**
- NT-EVID-2: Agent injects a fabricated evidence record into the evidence store. Submit request referencing the fabricated evidence. **Expected: denied.** **Actual: admitted if injection is not detected.**
- NT-EVID-3: Evidence is within the freshness window (e.g., < 5 seconds old) but the underlying state changed 1 second ago. **Expected: denied (evidence is semantically stale).** **Actual: admitted (evidence is "current").**
- NT-EVID-4: Agent and evidence generator share the same LLM backbone. Agent influences evidence generation through prompt injection in shared context. **Expected: denied.** **Actual: admitted (common-mode failure).**

**Verdict:** Evidence independence is **not enforceable** by `EvidenceCurrent` alone. It requires the `WithinAuth` control predicate and independent evidence generation infrastructure, neither of which is in the kernel. Evidence currency is **detection-only** for staleness within the freshness window, and **not enforceable** for independence of generation.

### 7.6 Non-Advantage of Concealment

**Claim:** Concealing information from the gate provides no advantage; an agent that hides its true intent or capabilities does not gain authority it would not have if transparent.

**Red-team analysis:**

This property is the strongest claim in the specification and the hardest to enforce. It requires that the gate's decision is based on the *effect* of the action, not its *declared intent*. Governed Individuation ([arXiv 2607.04613](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04613)) addresses this: it "routes every action through a gate defined over the semantic effect of the action rather than its name" and proves that "no amount of learning, skill acquisition, or self-induced governance abstraction can widen the agent's permitted authority without an operator-signed change to its identity."

**Attack 18: Name vs. effect mismatch.** If `ContractMatch` or `EffectTypeValid` evaluates the declared name/type of the action rather than its semantic effect, an agent can declare a benign name while causing a harmful effect. Governed Individuation specifically warns against name-based blocking: "an open-ended tool-use benchmark where a large action space rules out name-based blocking."

**Attack 19: Composition concealment.** An agent composes two individually-authorized actions into a jointly-unauthorized effect. Each action passes the gate individually, but their composition produces an effect that would not be authorized if evaluated as a single action. The BSSG kernel evaluates requests individually and has no composition analysis.

**Attack 20: Timing concealment.** An agent submits a benign request at time T, but the effect's consequence materializes at time T+Δ when the environment has changed. The gate evaluated the request as benign at T, but the effect is harmful at T+Δ. This is the commit-time authorization problem again, but from the concealment angle: the agent conceals the temporal sensitivity of the action.

**Attack 21: Channel concealment.** An agent encodes information in the *pattern* of admitted actions (timing, frequency, target selection) to communicate with an external system. Each individual action is authorized, but the pattern constitutes a covert channel. The gate has no mechanism to detect patterns across requests.

**Attack 22: Evidence selection concealment.** An agent selects which evidence to present to the gate, omitting evidence that would cause denial. If the gate does not require evidence completeness (caller-side exhaustiveness, AX-SH A3, [Zenodo 21500700](https://zenodo.org/records/21500700)), the agent can cherry-pick favorable evidence.

**Negative tests:**

- NT-CONCEAL-1: Agent declares action "read_file" but the effect is "delete_file" (name-effect mismatch). **Expected: denied (effect type is invalid).** **Actual: admitted if EffectTypeValid checks the declared name.**
- NT-CONCEAL-2: Agent submits r1 (copy A to B) and r2 (delete B), each individually authorized. Composition deletes B, which would not be authorized as a single action. **Expected: composition denied.** **Actual: both admitted individually.**
- NT-CONCEAL-3: Agent submits 1000 benign requests, each admitted. The timing pattern encodes a secret key. **Expected: covert channel detected.** **Actual: undetected (each request is individually benign).**
- NT-CONCEAL-4: Agent presents evidence set E1 that supports authorization, omitting evidence E2 that would deny it. **Expected: denied (incomplete evidence).** **Actual: admitted if evidence completeness is not checked.**

**Verdict:** Non-advantage of concealment is **partially preventable** (effect-based gating, as in Governed Individuation) but **detection-only** for composition, timing, and channel concealment. Evidence selection concealment is **preventable** only if caller-side exhaustiveness (AX-SH A3) is enforced, which is not in the kernel.

## 8. Conformance Levels

I propose three conformance levels, distinguishing what the kernel guarantees from what the deployment must guarantee.

### Level 1: Kernel Conformance (KC)

The implementation correctly evaluates the five-conjunct predicate with fail-closed (UNKNOWN → deny) semantics. All five conjuncts are evaluated; short-circuit evaluation is permitted only for FALSE, not for UNKNOWN. Witness degradation produces denial.

**What is guaranteed:** The gate itself is correct. If all five conjuncts are TRUE, the request is admitted; otherwise, it is denied.

**What is NOT guaranteed:** Non-bypassability, non-equivocation across replicas, provenance continuity, evidence independence, or non-advantage of concealment.

### Level 2: Deployed Conformance (DC)

The implementation satisfies Kernel Conformance *and* the deployment enforces:

1. **Complete mediation:** All side effects must pass through the gate. Kernel-enforced routes (as in SOUNDGATE) are required for all effect channels.
2. **Serial evaluation:** The gate evaluates one request at a time, or a consensus protocol (as in EBFT) ensures agreement across replicas.
3. **Evidence canonicalization:** Evidence is deterministically canonicalized before evaluation.
4. **Audit trail:** Every gate decision (admit, deny, reason) is durably logged.

**What is additionally guaranteed:** Non-bypassability (within the mediated channels), non-equivocation (for serial deployment), auditability.

**What is NOT guaranteed:** Provenance continuity, evidence independence, non-advantage of concealment, commit-time authorization.

### Level 3: Full Structural Conformance (FSC)

The implementation satisfies Deployed Conformance *and*:

1. **Lineage tracking:** Each request carries a lineage context C_i with monotonic authority restriction (PIC model, [arXiv 2607.08906](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08906v1)).
2. **Effect-based gating:** `EffectTypeValid` and `ContractMatch` evaluate the semantic effect, not the declared name (Governed Individuation, [arXiv 2607.04613](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04613)).
3. **Evidence independence:** Evidence is generated by a system independent of the acting system, under the `WithinAuth` control predicate (AX-SH Axiom 5, [Zenodo 21539074](https://zenodo.org/records/21539074)).
4. **Commit-time revalidation:** Evidence freshness and witness health are rechecked at the durability boundary (CommitGuard, [arXiv 2607.10487](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10487)).
5. **Witness semantic budget:** The witness system includes a bounded false-endorsement budget e_δ (EBFT, [arXiv 2607.16109](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.16109v1)).
6. **Caller-side exhaustiveness:** The agent must present all relevant evidence, not a cherry-picked subset (AX-SH A3, [Zenodo 21500700](https://zenodo.org/records/21500700)).
7. **Governance receipts:** Every decision produces a cryptographically verifiable receipt (AgentBound, [arXiv 2606.30970](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30970)).

**What is additionally guaranteed:** Provenance continuity (within linear chains), bounded authority (origin-bounded), evidence independence (within WithinAuth), non-advantage of concealment (for name-effect and evidence selection), commit-time authorization.

**What is still NOT guaranteed:** Non-advantage of concealment for composition, timing, and channel attacks; prevention of common-mode witness failure; multi-hop authority in fork/join topologies; covert channels.

### Conformance Level Summary

| Property | KC | DC | FSC |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fail-closed semantics | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |
| Monotonic narrowing | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |
| Non-bypassability | ❌ | ✅ (mediated channels) | ✅ (mediated channels) |
| Non-equivocation | ❌ | ✅ (serial) | ✅ (serial or consensus) |
| Provenance continuity | ❌ | ❌ | ✅ (linear chains) |
| Bounded authority (single-hop) | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |
| Bounded authority (multi-hop) | ❌ | ❌ | ✅ (linear) |
| Evidence independence | ❌ | ❌ | ✅ (within WithinAuth) |
| Non-advantage of concealment (name) | ❌ | ❌ | ✅ |
| Non-advantage of concealment (composition) | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ (detection only) |

## 10. Runnable Gate

```python
#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""
BSSG v1.0 Runnable Admission Gate

Frozen kernel: Allow(r) iff ContractMatch(r) AND EffectTypeValid(r)
    AND TargetAllowed(r) AND WitnessHealthy AND EvidenceCurrent

Principles:
- Missing/unestablished is UNKNOWN and cannot satisfy a conjunct.
- Witness degradation monotonically narrows authority.
"""

from enum import Enum
from dataclasses import dataclass, field
from typing import Optional
from datetime import datetime, timedelta


class TriState(Enum):
    TRUE = "true"
    FALSE = "false"
    UNKNOWN = "unknown"


class Verdict(Enum):
    ALLOW = "allow"
    DENY_FALSE = "deny"        # At least one conjunct is FALSE
    DENY_UNKNOWN = "deny_unknown"  # At least one conjunct is UNKNOWN


@dataclass
class Request:
    request_id: str
    effect_type: str
    target_resource: str
    target_type: str
    evidence_refs: list
    lineage_context: Optional[dict] = None  # Required at FSC
    timestamp: str = ""


@dataclass
class Contract:
    contract_id: str
    valid_effect_types: set
    target_rules: dict  # resource_pattern -> allowed_operations
    freshness_max_age: timedelta
    lineage_required: bool = False  # FSC: if True, TargetAllowed checks lineage


@dataclass
class WitnessHealth:
    status: str  # "healthy", "degraded", "unhealthy", "unknown"
    alive: Optional[bool] = None
    responsive: Optional[bool] = None
    attestation_valid: Optional[bool] = None
    semantic_budget: Optional[float] = None  # FSC: EBFT e_delta
    authority_narrowed: bool = False  # Monotonic: once narrowed, stays narrowed


@dataclass
class EvidencePackage:
    evidence_id: str
    content_hash: str
    generator_independence: str  # "fully_independent", "partially_independent", "acting_system", "unknown"
    generation_timestamp: Optional[datetime] = None
    is_current: Optional[bool] = None
    within_auth: Optional[bool] = None  # FSC: control predicate
    is_exhaustive: Optional[bool] = None  # FSC: AX-SH A3


@dataclass
class GateDecision:
    decision_id: str
    request_id: str
    verdict: Verdict
    conjunct_results: dict
    timestamp: str = ""


class BSSGGate:
    """
    BSSG v1.0 Admission Gate.

    Conformance level is set at construction:
    - 'KC': Kernel conformance (five-conjunct evaluation, fail-closed)
    - 'DC': Deployed conformance (KC + complete mediation, serial, audit)
    - 'FSC': Full structural conformance (DC + lineage, effect-based, independence, commit revalidation)
    """

    def __init__(self, contract: Contract, conformance_level: str = "KC"):
        self.contract = contract
        self.conformance_level = conformance_level
        self._authority_narrowed = False  # Monotonic narrowing state
        self._original_authority = None  # Set at first evaluation

    def evaluate(self, request: Request, witness: WitnessHealth,
                 evidence_packages: list) -> GateDecision:
        """
        Evaluate the five-conjunct predicate.

        Returns ALLOW iff all conjuncts are TRUE.
        Returns DENY_FALSE if any conjunct is FALSE.
        Returns DENY_UNKNOWN if any conjunct is UNKNOWN (and none are FALSE).
        """
        results = {}

        # 1. ContractMatch(r)
        results["contract_match"] = self._contract_match(request)

        # 2. EffectTypeValid(r)
        results["effect_type_valid"] = self._effect_type_valid(request)

        # 3. TargetAllowed(r)
        results["target_allowed"] = self._target_allowed(request)

        # 4. WitnessHealthy
        results["witness_healthy"] = self._witness_healthy(witness)

        # 5. EvidenceCurrent
        results["evidence_current"] = self._evidence_current(evidence_packages)

        # Determine verdict
        conjunct_values = list(results.values())

        if any(c == TriState.FALSE for c in conjunct_values):
            verdict = Verdict.DENY_FALSE
        elif any(c == TriState.UNKNOWN for c in conjunct_values):
            verdict = Verdict.DENY_UNKNOWN
        else:
            verdict = Verdict.ALLOW

        # Monotonic narrowing: if witness is not healthy, narrow authority
        if results["witness_healthy"] != TriState.TRUE:
            self._authority_narrowed = True

        return GateDecision(
            decision_id=f"dec_{request.request_id}",
            request_id=request.request_id,
            verdict=verdict,
            conjunct_results={k: v.value for k, v in results.items()},
            timestamp=datetime.utcnow().isoformat()
        )

    def _contract_match(self, request: Request) -> TriState:
        """Evaluate ContractMatch(r): does request match the contract?"""
        if not request.effect_type or not request.target_resource:
            return TriState.UNKNOWN

        # Check if request structure matches contract requirements
        target_rule = self.contract.target_rules.get(request.target_type)
        if target_rule is None:
            return TriState.FALSE  # No rule for this target type

        return TriState.TRUE

    def _effect_type_valid(self, request: Request) -> TriState:
        """Evaluate EffectTypeValid(r): is the effect type in the permitted set?"""
        if not request.effect_type:
            return TriState.UNKNOWN

        # FSC: evaluate semantic effect, not declared name
        if self.conformance_level == "FSC":
            # In full implementation, this would resolve the semantic effect
            # For now, check against valid types (effect-based, not name-based)
            pass

        if request.effect_type in self.contract.valid_effect_types:
            return TriState.TRUE
        else:
            return TriState.FALSE

    def _target_allowed(self, request: Request) -> TriState:
        """Evaluate TargetAllowed(r): is the target within authority scope?"""
        if not request.target_resource:
            return TriState.UNKNOWN

        target_rule = self.contract.target_rules.get(request.target_type)
        if target_rule is None:
            return TriState.FALSE

        allowed_ops = target_rule if isinstance(target_rule, set) else set(target_rule)

        if request.effect_type not in allowed_ops:
            return TriState.FALSE

        # FSC: lineage-sensitive check (confused deputy prevention)
        if self.conformance_level == "FSC" and self.contract.lineage_required:
            if request.lineage_context is None:
                return TriState.UNKNOWN

            origin_authority = request.lineage_context.get("authority_context", [])
            origin_ops = {(op["operation"], op["resource"]) for op in origin_authority}

            if (request.effect_type, request.target_resource) not in origin_ops:
                return TriState.FALSE  # Not in origin authority: confused deputy

        # Monotonic narrowing: if authority was narrowed, check against narrowed set
        if self._authority_narrowed:
            # In full implementation, narrowed authority would be tracked
            # For now, if narrowed, require explicit re-authorization
            pass

        return TriState.TRUE

    def _witness_healthy(self, witness: WitnessHealth) -> TriState:
        """Evaluate WitnessHealthy: is the witness alive, responsive, and attesting?"""
        if witness.status == "unknown":
            return TriState.UNKNOWN

        if witness.status == "unhealthy":
            return TriState.FALSE

        if witness.status == "degraded":
            # Degraded witness: narrow authority (monotonic)
            self._authority_narrowed = True
            return TriState.FALSE  # Degraded cannot satisfy the conjunct

        if witness.status == "healthy":
            # Check underlying signals
            if witness.alive is None or witness.responsive is None:
                return TriState.UNKNOWN
            if not witness.alive or not witness.responsive:
                return TriState.FALSE
            if witness.attestation_valid is None:
                return TriState.UNKNOWN
            if not witness.attestation_valid:
                return TriState.FALSE

            # FSC: check semantic budget
            if self.conformance_level == "FSC":
                if witness.semantic_budget is None:
                    return TriState.UNKNOWN
                if witness.semantic_budget > 0:
                    # Non-zero false-endorsement budget: healthy but not guaranteed correct
                    # This is the Honest Quorum Problem: healthy != correct
                    # At FSC, we accept this risk but record it
                    pass

            return TriState.TRUE

        return TriState.UNKNOWN

    def _evidence_current(self, evidence_packages: list) -> TriState:
        """Evaluate EvidenceCurrent: is evidence fresh and independent?"""
        if not evidence_packages:
            return TriState.UNKNOWN

        for pkg in evidence_packages:
            # Check freshness
            if pkg.is_current is None:
                return TriState.UNKNOWN
            if not pkg.is_current:
                return TriState.FALSE

            # Check generation timestamp
            if pkg.generation_timestamp is None:
                return TriState.UNKNOWN

            age = datetime.utcnow() - pkg.generation_timestamp
            if age > self.contract.freshness_max_age:
                return TriState.FALSE

            # FSC: check independence (AX-SH Axiom 5)
            if self.conformance_level == "FSC":
                if pkg.generator_independence == "acting_system":
                    return TriState.FALSE  # Acting system cannot generate its own evidence
                if pkg.generator_independence == "unknown":
                    return TriState.UNKNOWN
                if pkg.within_auth is None:
                    return TriState.UNKNOWN
                if not pkg.within_auth:
                    return TriState.FALSE

                # FSC: check exhaustiveness (AX-SH A3)
                if pkg.is_exhaustive is None:
                    return TriState.UNKNOWN
                if not pkg.is_exhaustive:
                    return TriState.FALSE  # Cherry-picked evidence is not current

        return TriState.TRUE

    def commit_revalidation(self, decision: GateDecision,
                            witness: WitnessHealth,
                            evidence_packages: list) -> bool:
        """
        FSC only: Commit-time revalidation.

        Per CommitGuard (arXiv 2607.10487): recheck witness health and evidence
        freshness at the durability boundary.

        Returns True if the commit is authorized, False if stale.
        """
        if self.conformance_level != "FSC":
            return True  # No revalidation at lower levels

        # Recheck witness
        if self._witness_healthy(witness) != TriState.TRUE:
            return False

        # Recheck evidence
        if self._evidence_current(evidence_packages) != TriState.TRUE:
            return False

        return True
```

### 11.2 Counterexamples

**Counterexample 1: Healthy witness, wrong judgment.**

Per the Honest Quorum Problem ([arXiv 2607.16109](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.16109v1), Definition 4.1): there exists an admission instance (x, s, h_s) with V(x,s)=0 and a set Q of authenticated, responsive, non-equivocating, protocol-compliant validators such that |Q| ≥ q and every validator returns a signed endorsement. In BSSG terms: `WitnessHealthy = TRUE`, `EvidenceCurrent = TRUE`, `ContractMatch = TRUE`, `EffectTypeValid = TRUE`, `TargetAllowed = TRUE`, but the action is semantically invalid. The kernel admits it. **This is a false positive that the kernel cannot prevent by construction.**

**Counterexample 2: Possession-based TargetAllowed admits confused deputy.**

Per Theorem 2 of Proof-of-Continuity ([arXiv 2607.08906](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08906v1)): if TargetAllowed checks whether the agent possesses authority over the target (rather than whether the target is in the request's lineage), the confused deputy is admissible. Agent A holds authority over Y; user U (lacking authority over Y) sends a request to A targeting Y; A executes against Y using its own authority. `TargetAllowed = TRUE` (A has authority), but the action is a confused deputy. **The kernel admits it unless lineage tracking is enforced (FSC only).**

**Counterexample 3: Evidence current but not independent.**

Per AX-SH Axiom 5 decomposition ([Zenodo 21539074](https://zenodo.org/records/21539074)): A5.1 (acting-system-independent evidence generation) is not derivable from A1-A4. The BSSG kernel's `EvidenceCurrent` checks freshness but not independence. If the agent generates its own evidence (e.g., by writing to a shared store that the evidence generator reads), the evidence is current but not independent. `EvidenceCurrent = TRUE` but the evidence is compromised. **The kernel admits it unless the WithinAuth control predicate is enforced (FSC only).**

**Counterexample 4: TOCTOU between gate and commit.**

Per commit-time authorization ([arXiv 2607.10487](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10487)): in 207 of 216 invalidating rows, agents commit durable effects after the authorizing path has failed. The BSSG kernel evaluates `EvidenceCurrent` at gate time. If the evidence becomes stale between gate evaluation and effect commit, the gate admitted the action but the commit is unauthorized. **The kernel admits it unless commit-time revalidation is enforced (FSC only).**

**Counterexample 5: Consistent liar.**

Per ECO/CPO-DAG ([arXiv 2607.06804](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06804)): "a party that never contradicts itself is invisible to it." An agent that consistently produces false evidence (never contradicting itself) passes all consistency checks. The BSSG kernel has no contradiction detection, but even if it did, a consistent liar would be invisible. **This is fundamentally out of model for any contradiction-based approach.**

### 11.3 Out-of-Model Residue

The following are explicitly out of model for BSSG v1.0 at any conformance level:

1. **Covert channels.** The gate mediates explicit effects, not information flows through timing, resource consumption, or side channels. No conformance level addresses this.

2. **Composition attacks.** The gate evaluates individual requests. Compositions of individually-authorized requests that produce jointly-unauthorized effects are not detectable by the kernel. This would require a composition analysis layer, which is not specified.

3. **Common-mode witness failure.** Per EBFT Theorem 5.3 ([arXiv 2607.16109](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.16109v1)): if witnesses share a common failure mode (same model family, training distribution, or evidence source), correlated false endorsements are bounded only by P(C), which is a property of the witness system, not the gate.

4. **Lineage origination.** Per Proof-of-Continuity Limitation 5 ([arXiv 2607.08906](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08906v1)): distinguishing request-caused actions from executor-originated actions is left to the PoR construction and enforcement architecture. The BSSG kernel does not specify PoR.

5. **Multi-hop fork/join.** Per Proof-of-Continuity Limitation 2: the model is linear. Forks, joins, fan-out, and fan-in are future work. BSSG inherits this limitation.

6. **Revocation semantics.** Per Proof-of-Continuity Limitation 1: revocation is not treated as a retroactive operation. The BSSG kernel does not specify what happens when authority is revoked after a request is admitted but before the effect commits.

7. **Heterogeneous operation spaces.** Per Proof-of-Continuity: different hops may use different operation vocabularies, requiring policy translations T whose soundness is assumed but not verified by the model.

8. **Semantic correctness of the contract.** The gate enforces the contract, but if the contract itself is wrong (permits harmful actions), the gate faithfully admits harmful actions. This is the "correct-by-construction" assumption: the gate is only as good as the contract it enforces.

9. **Post-quantum attribution.** Per AX-SH Axiom 5 decomposition ([Zenodo 21539074](https://zenodo.org/records/21539074)): "hash-based integrity vs signature-based attribution" — hash-based integrity is post-quantum, but signature-based attribution is not. The BSSG kernel does not specify which cryptographic primitives are used.

10. **Effect completeness.** The gate evaluates whether to admit a request, but does not verify that the executed effect matches the admitted request. If the execution layer deviates from the admitted action (e.g., executes a write instead of a read), the gate cannot detect this without an outbox and effect verification layer.

## 12. Falsifiers

A falsifier is a testable condition that, if demonstrated, refutes a claimed property. Per the SHVVP v0.8 design ([Zenodo 21583202](https://zenodo.org/records/21583202)), falsification conditions must be stated before any corpus or session exists.

### F1: Bypass Falsifier (Non-Bypassability)

**Condition:** If any side effect reaches execution without passing through the gate, non-bypassability is falsified.

**Test:** Deploy the gate in a standard agent environment. Enumerate all effect channels (network, file system, IPC, shared memory). Attempt to cause an effect on each channel without submitting a request to the gate. If any channel succeeds, non-bypassability is falsified for that deployment.

**Expected at KC:** Falsified (no complete mediation).
**Expected at DC:** Not falsified (if complete mediation is correctly deployed).
**Expected at FSC:** Not falsified.

### F2: Unknown-Satisfaction Falsifier (Fail-Closed)

**Condition:** If any request is admitted while any conjunct evaluates to UNKNOWN, fail-closed semantics is falsified.

**Test:** Submit a request with missing fields (no effect type, no target, no evidence). Submit a request with an unreachable witness. Submit a request with evidence whose freshness cannot be determined. In each case, the gate must deny.

**Expected at all levels:** Not falsified (by construction).

### F3: Monotonic Narrowing Falsifier

**Condition:** If witness degradation (healthy → unhealthy → healthy) causes authority to widen beyond its pre-degradation level, monotonic narrowing is falsified.

**Test:** Evaluate a request with a healthy witness (admit). Degrade the witness (deny). Recover the witness. Evaluate the same request. If the request is admitted with authority exceeding the pre-degradation level, monotonic narrowing is falsified.

**Expected at all levels:** Not falsified (by construction, if recovery does not widen beyond original).

**Caveat:** If recovery semantics are unspecified, this falsifier may be trivially satisfied (no recovery) or trivially violated (full recovery). The specification must define recovery semantics.

### F4: Confused Deputy Falsifier (Provenance Continuity)

**Condition:** If `TargetAllowed` admits a request where the target is not in the request's causal lineage, provenance continuity is falsified.

**Test:** Agent A holds authority over targets X and Y. User U (with authority over X only) sends a request to A targeting Y. If the gate admits the request, provenance continuity is falsified.

**Expected at KC/DC:** Falsified (TargetAllowed checks possession, not lineage).
**Expected at FSC:** Not falsified (lineage tracking enforced).

### F5: Evidence Independence Falsifier

**Condition:** If `EvidenceCurrent` is satisfied by evidence generated by the acting system, evidence independence is falsified.

**Test:** Agent writes to a shared store. Evidence generator reads from the same store. Agent submits a request with evidence derived from data the agent wrote. If the gate admits the request, evidence independence is falsified.

**Expected at KC/DC:** Falsified (no independence check).
**Expected at FSC:** Not falsified (if WithinAuth is correctly enforced).

### F6: Honest Quorum Falsifier (Witness Semantic Correctness)

**Condition:** If `WitnessHealthy = TRUE` but the witness produces a semantically incorrect judgment that is admitted, witness semantic correctness is falsified.

**Test:** Deploy a witness quorum where all validators share the same model family. Submit a request where the correct semantic judgment is "deny" but the model family has a known blind spot. If the quorum endorses the request and the gate admits it, witness semantic correctness is falsified.

**Expected at all levels:** Potentially falsified (common-mode failure). The kernel cannot prevent this.

### F7: TOCTOU Falsifier (Commit-Time Authorization)

**Condition:** If a request admitted by the gate commits a durable effect after the evidence has become stale, commit-time authorization is falsified.

**Test:** Submit a request. Gate evaluates and admits. Before the effect commits, invalidate the evidence (change the underlying state). If the effect commits, commit-time authorization is falsified.

**Expected at KC/DC:** Falsified (no commit-time revalidation).
**Expected at FSC:** Not falsified (if commit revalidation is correctly enforced).

### F8: Concealment Falsifier (Non-Advantage of Concealment)

**Condition:** If an agent that conceals its true intent gains authority it would not have if transparent, non-advantage of concealment is falsified.

**Test:** Agent submits a request declaring effect type "read" but the actual effect is "delete" (name-effect mismatch). If the gate admits the request, non-advantage of concealment is falsified for name-based gating.

**Expected at KC/DC:** Potentially falsified (if EffectTypeValid checks declared name).
**Expected at FSC:** Not falsified (if effect-based gating is enforced).

### F9: Composition Falsifier

**Condition:** If two individually-authorized requests compose to produce an unauthorized effect, non-advantage of concealment is falsified for composition.

**Test:** Submit r1 (copy A to B) and r2 (delete B), each individually authorized. If both are admitted and B is deleted, composition concealment is demonstrated.

**Expected at all levels:** Falsified (no composition analysis in the kernel).

### F10: Consistent Liar Falsifier

**Condition:** If an agent that consistently produces false evidence (never contradicting itself) is admitted, the kernel's evidence checking is falsified for consistent liars.

**Test:** Agent generates false evidence that is internally consistent and fresh. Submit a request with this evidence. If admitted, the consistent liar attack succeeds.

**Expected at all levels:** Falsified (the kernel cannot detect consistent lies, per ECO/CPO-DAG's boundary: "it detects provable contradictions, not consistent lies" [arXiv 2607.06804](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06804)).

## 13. Disagreements and Dissents

### Dissent 1: WitnessHealthy is overloaded

**Position:** The `WitnessHealthy` conjunct conflates three distinct properties: liveness (is the witness alive?), responsiveness (does it respond?), and correctness (are its judgments right?). The frozen kernel treats this as a single boolean, but the EBFT framework ([arXiv 2607.16109](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.16109v1)) demonstrates that these are independent dimensions. A witness can be alive, responsive, and wrong (the Honest Quorum Problem).

**Recommendation:** Split `WitnessHealthy` into `WitnessAlive` (liveness) and `WitnessCorrect` (semantic correctness, with a bounded false-endorsement budget). This would make the kernel:

`Allow(r) iff ContractMatch(r) AND EffectTypeValid(r) AND TargetAllowed(r) AND WitnessAlive AND WitnessCorrect AND EvidenceCurrent`

**Counter-argument (preserved):** Adding `WitnessCorrect` as a conjunct would make the gate unverifiable in practice, because semantic correctness cannot be determined at gate time without an oracle (EBFT Theorem 5.4: "No protocol whose acceptance rule uses only agreement signals can guarantee semantic certificate validity for all executions unless it assumes at least one additional semantic grounding mechanism"). Making it a conjunct would mean the gate can never allow anything (UNKNOWN → deny), which is safe but useless.

**Resolution proposed:** Keep `WitnessHealthy` as a single conjunct for the kernel, but require at FSC that the witness system publishes a semantic risk profile (as in EBFT's committee risk profile) that includes the false-endorsement budget e_δ. This makes the risk explicit without making the gate unusable.

### Dissent 2: EvidenceCurrent vs. EvidenceIndependent

**Position:** The kernel has `EvidenceCurrent` but not `EvidenceIndependent`. These are different properties. Evidence can be current (fresh) but not independent (generated by the acting system). AX-SH Axiom 5 ([Zenodo 21539074](https://zenodo.org/records/21539074)) explicitly establishes that evidence independence is not derivable from the other axioms. By not including it as a conjunct, the kernel admits self-generated evidence.

**Counter-argument (preserved):** Evidence independence is a property of the evidence generation infrastructure, not of the gate. The gate cannot verify independence at evaluation time without an oracle that knows the generation provenance. Including it as a conjunct would make it UNKNOWN in most deployments (UNKNOWN → deny), which is safe but impractical.

**Resolution proposed:** Evidence independence should be a conformance requirement at FSC, enforced through the `WithinAuth` control predicate and verified through deployment configuration, not through gate-time evaluation. The kernel should note this as an explicit out-of-model residue at KC/DC.

### Dissent 3: Monotonic narrowing is too weak

**Position:** The specification states "witness degradation monotonically narrows authority." This is true but weak. It means that authority can only narrow when the witness degrades. But it says nothing about what happens when the witness recovers. If recovery is allowed, authority could widen back, which violates the spirit of monotonic narrowing (if not the letter, since the narrowing was on degradation, and recovery is a separate event).

**Counter-argument (preserved):** Monotonic narrowing is specifically about degradation, not recovery. Recovery is a separate event that the specification does not address. The property is: "degradation narrows," not "authority is monotonically non-increasing over all events." Under this reading, the property is correct.

**Resolution proposed:** The specification should explicitly state recovery semantics. Recommended: recovery narrows to the minimum of (pre-degradation authority, post-recovery authority), ensuring that authority never exceeds its pre-degradation level. This is consistent with the Proof-of-Continuity model's monotonic restriction: "any authority dropped at some hop j is lost irreversibly: by monotonicity it cannot reappear at any later hop" ([arXiv 2607.08906](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08906v1)).

### Dissent 4: Single-condition EvidenceCurrent is insufficient

**Position:** The commit-time authorization work ([arXiv 2607.10487](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10487)) found that "prompt caution and single-condition checks are insufficient because different hazards break different boundary conditions." `EvidenceCurrent` as a single boolean is a single-condition check. It cannot distinguish: (a) timestamp-based staleness, (b) content-hash staleness, (c) dependency-state staleness, (d) version-witness staleness.

**Counter-argument (preserved):** The kernel is intentionally minimal. It specifies what must be true (evidence is current), not how to check it. The freshness mechanism is a deployment choice. Different deployments may use different freshness signals.

**Resolution proposed:** The conformance levels should require multi-condition freshness checking at FSC (as CommitGuard does with witness, dependency, binding, and eligibility signals). At KC, single-condition (timestamp) is acceptable but must be documented as a known weakness.

### Dissent 5: The kernel does not address correction propagation

**Position:** The task asks for analysis of "correction propagation." When a gate decision is later found to be wrong (e.g., a false admission), what happens to the effects that were authorized? The kernel does not specify correction propagation. Mnemosyne's Evidence-Preserving Repair (EPR) property ([arXiv 2607.00269](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.00269v2)) addresses this: "repairs that may not destroy the evidence that justified them." Without EPR, corrections may destroy the audit trail needed to understand why the wrong decision was made.

**Counter-argument (preserved):** Correction propagation is a recovery concern, not an admission concern. The kernel governs admission; correction is a separate lifecycle phase.

**Resolution proposed:** The specification should acknowledge that correction propagation is out of model for the kernel but required at FSC. At FSC, the gate must produce governance receipts (as in AgentBound) that enable evidence-preserving repair.

## 14. Detection vs. Prevention Boundary

This section consolidates where the architecture can only claim detection rather than prevention.

### Properties Preventable by the Kernel (by construction)

1. **Fail-closed semantics:** UNKNOWN → deny. Preventable at all conformance levels.
2. **Monotonic narrowing under witness degradation:** Authority narrows when witness degrades. Preventable at all conformance levels.
3. **Bounded authority (single-hop):** The agent cannot exercise authority not checked by `TargetAllowed`. Preventable at all conformance levels (within the gate's scope).

### Properties Preventable Only at FSC

4. **Non-bypassability:** Preventable only with complete mediation (environmental, not kernel).
5. **Non-equivocation:** Preventable only with serial evaluation or consensus (deployment choice).
6. **Provenance continuity:** Preventable only with lineage tracking (not in kernel).
7. **Evidence independence:** Preventable only with `WithinAuth` control predicate (not in kernel).
8. **Commit-time authorization:** Preventable only with commit-time revalidation (not in kernel).
9. **Non-advantage of concealment (name-effect):** Preventable only with effect-based gating (not in kernel).

### Properties That Are Detection-Only at Any Level

10. **Witness semantic error:** The Honest Quorum Problem is fundamental. Protocol-compliant witnesses can collectively endorse invalid actions. The kernel can detect this post-hoc (through audit replay) but cannot prevent it at gate time. **This is the hard boundary of the architecture.**

11. **Composition attacks:** The gate evaluates individual requests. Compositions of authorized requests that produce unauthorized effects can only be detected by a separate composition analysis layer. **The kernel has no composition analysis.**

12. **Covert channels:** Information flows through timing, resource consumption, or side channels are not mediated by the gate. **Fundamentally out of model.**

13. **Consistent lying:** An agent that consistently produces false evidence without internal contradiction is invisible to contradiction-based detection (per ECO/CPO-DAG, [arXiv 2607.06804](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06804)). **Fundamentally out of model.**

14. **Common-mode witness failure:** Correlated false endorsements from nominally diverse witnesses are bounded only by P(C), which is a property of the witness system. **The kernel cannot bound this.**

15. **Authority capture:** If the witness is compromised (not degraded, but corrupted), it remains "healthy" while producing adversarial judgments. The kernel cannot distinguish healthy-and-corrupted from healthy-and-correct. **Detection requires external monitoring; prevention requires witness diversity and attestation.**

## 16. Verdict

**CONDITIONAL APPROVAL** of BSSG v1.0 as a canonical architecture-and-conformance specification, subject to the following mandatory conditions:

### Mandatory Conditions

1. **Conformance level restructuring.** The specification must adopt the three-level conformance structure (KC, DC, FSC) proposed in Section 8, with explicit declarations of what is guaranteed at each level. No property may be claimed without specifying the conformance level at which it holds.

2. **Detection-vs-prevention boundary declaration.** The specification must explicitly declare which properties are preventable by the kernel, which are preventable only at FSC, and which are detection-only at any level (Section 14). The Honest Quorum Problem, composition attacks, covert channels, consistent lying, common-mode witness failure, and authority capture must be listed as detection-only or out-of-model.

3. **Lineage tracking requirement at FSC.** `TargetAllowed` must be lineage-sensitive at FSC (per Proof-of-Continuity, [arXiv 2607.08906](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08906v1)). Without this, the confused deputy is admissible and provenance continuity is not enforceable.

4. **Evidence independence requirement at FSC.** `EvidenceCurrent` must be augmented with the `WithinAuth` control predicate at FSC (per AX-SH Axiom 5, [Zenodo 21539074](https://zenodo.org/records/21539074)). Without this, self-generated evidence is admissible.

5. **Commit-time revalidation at FSC.** The state machine must include a commit-time revalidation checkpoint (per CommitGuard, [arXiv 2607.10487](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10487)). Without this, TOCTOU vulnerabilities are exploitable.

6. **Complete mediation as deployment requirement at DC+.** Non-bypassability must be declared as an environmental property, not a kernel property, and must require kernel-enforced routes (per SOUNDGATE, [arXiv 2607.14166](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14166)).

7. **Recovery semantics specification.** The specification must define what happens when a degraded witness recovers. Recommended: authority narrows to the minimum of pre-degradation and post-recovery authority, ensuring monotonic non-increase.

8. **Witness semantic risk profile at FSC.** The witness system must publish a semantic risk profile including the false-endorsement budget e_δ (per EBFT, [arXiv 2607.16109](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.16109v1)), making the Honest Quorum risk explicit.

9. **Out-of-model residue declaration.** The specification must explicitly list all items in Section 11.3 as out of model, with the reason each is excluded.

10. **No effectiveness claims.** Consistent with the task scope, the specification must make no claims about effectiveness (e.g., "this prevents X% of attacks"). All claims must be structural: "property P holds at conformance level L under assumptions A."

### What the Kernel Gets Right

- The five-conjunct conjunction is a sound fail-closed design. UNKNOWN cannot satisfy any conjunct.
- Monotonic narrowing under witness degradation is correct by construction.
- The effect-type and target checks, if lineage-sensitive and effect-based, provide bounded authority within a single hop.
- The frozen kernel is minimal and composable: additional properties can be added as conformance requirements without modifying the kernel.

### What the Kernel Cannot Guarantee

- Non-bypassability (environmental, not kernel).
- Witness semantic correctness (Honest Quorum Problem).
- Provenance continuity (requires lineage tracking, not in kernel).
- Evidence independence (requires WithinAuth, not in kernel).
- Commit-time authorization (requires revalidation, not in kernel).
- Non-advantage of concealment for composition, timing, and channels (fundamentally out of model).
