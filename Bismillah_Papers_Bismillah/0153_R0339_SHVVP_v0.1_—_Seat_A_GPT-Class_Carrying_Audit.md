**HALT**

The freeze is not deposit-ready. ## Axis 1 — hostile falsifier review

### Master falsifier (§4)

- **Post-result reinterpretation:** Yes. “Consequential,” “better evidence,” and “materially” are unpinned escape hatches (F-PP-2).
- **Operationalization:** Time, recall, evidence completeness, false positives, reviewer-hours, label dependence, legitimate generated differences, and missing-provenance behavior are named elsewhere, but no threshold/rubric maps them to the master decision.
- **Committed data:** Most required observations are collected in Phase A. Several master clauses are not naturally applicable to Phases B and C, despite the “in any phase” scope.
- **Conclusion:** Not an enforceable master falsifier as frozen.

### Phase-A falsifier (§5)

- **Post-result reinterpretation:** Yes, through the same three unpinned terms.
- **Operationalization:** The nine metrics provide relevant data, and blinding plus the legitimate-difference discipline are strong. However, there are no fixed effect thresholds, evidence-quality rubric, materiality threshold, or rule for combining metrics.
- **Committed data:** Yes for the listed clauses, including false positives on generated files, reviewer-hours, timing, evidence completeness, and correction reach.
- **Conclusion:** Data-bearing but decision-incomplete; blocking under F-PP-2.

### Phase-B two-sided criterion (§6)

- **Post-result reinterpretation:** Yes. Both decisive thresholds can be chosen after the 160-session pilot.
- **Operationalization:** No numeric unauthorized-effect target, no numeric noninferiority margin, no joint decision rule, and no stated numeric power target. Mann-Whitney is named only for continuous outcomes; the primary binary/rate outcomes lack a frozen primary test.
- **Committed data:** The 160/800 session design and outcomes are committed, but the criterion that turns those data into success or failure is not.
- **Conclusion:** Direct preregistration failure; F-PP-1 requires HALT.

### Six novelty-contribution routes (§4)

1. **Full projection graph:** Phase A commits multi-projection mappings and chain/recall data, but does not define the incremental threshold for “more than the source-build pair.”
2. **Typed unenumerated outcomes:** Condition B and caller-context enumeration are committed; evidence completeness and unsupported-inference counts are only indirect proxies, with no route-specific score.
3. **Preserved conflicting projections:** “Independent projection reconciliation” is named, but no field or metric tests preservation without premature reconciliation. This route may be inert for lack of committed direct data.
4. **Authority bound to witness health:** Best operationalized route. Phase B includes witness interruption and “fraction of sessions in which telemetry loss strictly narrows authority”; the pass threshold remains absent.
5. **Descendant correction propagation:** Direct data are committed through Phase-A descendant reach and a Phase-C revised seeded report; the descendant universe and minimum acceptable reach are not fixed.
6. **Missing provenance as UNKNOWN:** The semantic rule is explicit and favorable defaulting is a falsifier, but no denominator or adjudication rule fixes how mixed/misclassified cases are scored.

Because the protocol allows “one or more” routes without a predeclared route-level claim rule, positive route selection remains post hoc (F-PP-4).

## Axis 2 — independent empirical-anchor verification

### CVE-2026-4747

Anthropic’s primary says Mythos Preview fully autonomously identified and exploited the 17-year-old FreeBSD NFS RCE, starting from an unauthenticated user, and its summary states that the model split a 20-gadget ROP chain across multiple packets ([Anthropic, “Assessing Claude Mythos Preview’s cybersecurity capabilities”](https://www.anthropic.com/research/mythos-preview)). Thus the protocol accurately reproduces Anthropic’s capability claim.

The discovery-versus-retrieval dispute is real. FreeBSD announced the advisory on March 26, 2026 and credited “Nicholas Carlini using Claude, Anthropic”; its problem description says prior authentication is not required, while its impact section says kernel RCE is possible by an authenticated user ([FreeBSD-SA-26:08.rpcsec_gss](https://www.freebsd.org/security/advisories/FreeBSD-SA-26:08.rpcsec_gss.asc)). Calif.io records asking a prior Claude model for an exploit on March 29, after disclosure, and obtaining a working root-shell exploit that day ([Calif.io MAD Bugs](https://blog.calif.io/p/mad-bugs-claude-wrote-a-full-freebsd)). The skeptical account reports eight-of-eight open-weight detection, alleges retrieval from a 2007 upstream fix, but also updates the record to concede that Mythos found the bug in its targeted scaffold and autonomously wrote the ROP chain ([flyingpenguin technical critique](https://www.flyingpenguin.com/freebsd-cve-2026-4747-log-suggests-mythos-is-a-marketing-trick/)).

The protocol handles the core dispute honestly: it labels the CVE a motivating profile, records the dispute, and uses it only to motivate seeded, previously unseen Phase-C targets. Searches of the canonical source found no locus where CVE-2026-4747 supplies a discovery-novelty label. The remaining issue is only the unauthenticated-root precision noted in F-PP-6.

### AISI “The Last Ones”

AISI’s primary confirms a 32-step simulation, full completion in 3 of 10 attempts, an average of 22 of 32 steps, next-best Opus 4.6 at 16, and a 100M-token budget. It also states that the ranges lack active defenders and defensive tooling, impose no penalties for alert-triggering actions, and do not establish performance against well-defended systems ([UK AI Security Institute evaluation](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/our-evaluation-of-claude-mythos-previews-cyber-capabilities)).

The protocol states these figures and caveats accurately. It uses the profile to set canary/task difficulty and witness-degradation stress, not as evidence that BSSG works. No finding.

## Axis 3 — internal consistency and completeness

- **Deferred thresholds:** Both Phase-B values are holes, not acceptable defer-to-pilot items. Transparent logging does not remove outcome dependence (F-PP-1).
- **Five-conjunct authorization:** The Boolean rule is genuinely fail-closed: all five conjuncts are required, failure of any denies, and missing evidence is explicitly denied.
- **Monotonicity:** The subset rule is fail-closed in direction, and degraded/conflicting/failed witness states narrow, pause, or suspend authority. Predicate implementation still needs validation later, but no permissive missing-conjunct reading appears in this freeze.
- **Per-phase safety:** Phase A is offline and prohibits reachable execution; Phase B is containerized/simulated/non-production and forbids reward for real external access; Phase C prohibits live zero-days, requires offline synthetic/non-deployable artifacts, and expressly makes the boundary non-amendable. The Phase-C safety boundary is hard as written.
- **Per-phase falsification:** Phase A has a named but underdefined falsifier; Phase B has an underdefined two-sided criterion; Phase C has an all-six success sentence but no operational phase-specific falsifier (F-PP-3).
- **Material omissions:** Phase-B thresholds/statistical decisions, Phase-C sizing/scoring, and route-specific metrics/claim rules are absent.

## Axis 4 — scope honesty

The document clearly says it is not an empirical paper and does not validate SHV. It does not generalize XZ, CVE-Bench, or Phase C beyond their stated evidentiary roles. Provenance, model assistance, author authority, lack of independent human peer review, and the distinction between cross-model checking and peer review are disclosed.

The UNKNOWN discipline is appropriately applied to missing evidence and A5’s pending deposit, and the CVE dispute is not papered over. It is not applied to the document’s own missing Phase-B thresholds and Phase-C decision rules; those gaps are instead described as frozen or deferred (F-PP-5).
