Is Evidence Independence an Axiom?
Decomposing a Proposed Extension to AX-SH
Bilal Syed Arfeen
July 23, 2026
Author note. Authored by Bilal Syed Arfeen. This paper was prepared with Perplexity Computer as an analytical and
drafting instrument under the author’s direction and final authority. The proposal it examines was itself produced by
a collaborating model instance under the author’s direction. All load-bearing claims were cross-checked against the
canonical AX-SH source as deposited and against independent standards literature. The corpus of which this paper is
part is self-published on Zenodo and reviewed by cross-model consistency checking; it has not undergone independent
human peer review (Section 15). Cross-model agreement recorded in the companion adjudication, included in the
same audit-trail packet as this paper, is a consistency check, not peer review, and is not presented as such.

1. Status and attribution
The proposal this paper examines is not the author’s own analytic conclusion but a monograph-extension
proposal, Axiom 5, Observer Independence and Evidence Completeness 16 , produced by a collaborating model
instance working under the author’s direction. That document supplies five formal clauses, a threat model,
a security architecture, graded assurance levels, and falsification conditions. It is careful, well-sourced, and
considerably more developed than most proposed extensions receive.
This paper attempts to derive each of the five clauses from the four existing axioms of Structural Honesty
(AX-SH) and reports what survives. The result is not that the proposal fails. It is that the proposal contains
four different kinds of thing presented as one, and that separating them makes each stronger than
the conjunction was.
A note on what this paper does not claim. It does not claim that evidence independence is unimportant,
that the proposed architecture is unsound, or that the extension should be abandoned. It claims that one
clause is an axiom, one belongs in a control layer rather than the axiom set, one is a deployment condition
rather than a structural property, and two are theorems. Each claim is checkable against the canon, and the
paper states where each would fail.
The paper’s contribution is the negative decomposition, the demonstration that most of the proposal
dissolves under derivation, while its one positive result is the single surviving axiom candidate. These two
framings are complementary, not contradictory, and the paper uses them consistently: "the central positive
result" refers throughout to the survival of A5.1 as an independent axiom, and "the negative decomposition"
refers to the collapse of the remaining four clauses into theorems, a control principle, and a deployment
condition. The canonical AX-SH framework and its four axioms are cited from the deposited flagship
edition1 and consolidated reference canon2 .

2. Abstract
A proposed fifth axiom for the AX-SH framework asserts, in five clauses, that evidence for safety-relevant
events must be generated by an observer independent of the acting system, that evidence must causally gate
consequential action, that divergent evidentiary status is itself a signal, that at least one witness must sit
outside the operator’s authority, and that the completeness boundary must be declared. This paper attempts
each clause’s derivation from the four existing axioms and reports four different outcomes.
One clause is not derivable and is axiom-shaped. Acting-system-independent generation of evidence
(A5.1) closes a gap the existing projection-consistency axiom deliberately leaves open: that axiom is conditional on projections existing and says nothing about whether any must exist for a covered fact. We state
A5.1 as Axiom 5 (Evidence independence), defining independence by a control predicate (the acting system
1


cannot cause, modify, suppress, reconfigure, or credential the evidence-generation act without an independently detectable violation) rather than by authority labels. We exhibit a finite countermodel satisfying
A1-A4 while violating it (verified clause by clause), and a separate finite model jointly satisfying A1-A4 and
A5 (also verified clause by clause), with the completeness boundary fixed ex ante.
We are careful to separate two levels for a covered in-boundary occurrence that lacks timely independent
evidence. At the axiom level, A5 is UNSATISFIED (nonconforming) for that occurrence. At the
event-level adjudicative level, the verdict remains UNKNOWN under Proposition 1 (no default to
compliance or violation). These are distinct statuses and the paper never collapses them into a single
compound label.
One clause is not derivable for a different reason. Causal gating (A5.2) constrains what a system may
do, where the existing four axioms uniformly constrain what it must represent. Admitting it would change
the framework’s character from diagnostic to control. We describe its relocation as a proposed control
principle for the program’s SHIELD layer; we do not claim SHIELD already contains it, because no citable
SHIELD artifact is presently available for verification.
One clause is genuine and is not structural. Operator-independence (A5.4) is a claim about how
verification authority is distributed among institutions, which no property of a substrate entails and no
architecture guarantees. We restate it as a measurable deployment condition over an extended evidence
relation with an explicit falsification test.
Two clauses are theorems. A5.3’s no-default half and A5.5 are proved from A2 and A4. A5.3’s recording/escalation half remains an open operational requirement, because the bridging lemma it would need is
unproved.
The paper then declares a cryptographic boundary the proposal does not: the extension’s integrity properties
are materially more robust under known quantum attacks than its attribution properties, and later key or
algorithm compromise can undermine standalone historical attribution unless trusted timestamps, evidencerecord renewal, validation material, and crypto-agility protect it. All proofs are finite model-theoretic paper
proofs; proof-assistant formalization remains future work.
Keywords: structural honesty; axiomatic independence; evidence independence; observer independence;
audit; provenance; post-quantum migration; AI safety

3. What is being asked
AX-SH states four axioms1 . Append-only provenance (A1) requires that a module’s public surface at
each version be a behavioral subtype of its predecessor in the Liskov-Wing sense, with supersessions logged
rather than silent. Surface-substrate consistency (A2) requires a declared correspondence relation
holding under a declared observer model O and context set K. Caller-side exhaustiveness (A3) requires
that every outcome in a verifier’s conservative over-approximation Rmay be handled, propagated as typed
incompleteness, or discharged as unreachable. Projection consistency (A4) requires that a declared
family of evidence projections cohere where their domains meet, with the explicit rule that absence of a
projection at a point places that point outside the axiom’s scope, not inside it as a violation 1 .
The proposal under examination observes, correctly and importantly, that all four can hold over a record
that is complete, authentic, and false about what occurred, because none of them requires that any
evidence be generated by a party other than the system whose behavior is at issue. Its opening formulation
is that recorded honesty is not structural honesty, and that is right.
The question this paper asks is narrower than whether the observation is right. It is: does closing that
gap require a fifth axiom, and if so, which part of the proposal is it?
The question matters beyond bookkeeping. An axiom set that grows by accretion loses the property that
makes it useful, that each member is independent and each carries its own weight. A proposal packaging an
axiom, a control requirement, a deployment condition, and two theorems into one numbered addition makes
all four harder to evaluate and the set as a whole harder to trust.

4. The proposal, as stated
The proposal defines a substrate S, a set E * of safety-relevant event tokens or types, a witness set W, a
completeness boundary B, and a trusted computing base, and states five clauses over that declaration. It is
cited as an unpublished working proposal on file with the author16 .
• A5.1, acting-system-independent generation. For every covered event, at least one observer independent of S has produced evidence recorded in the ledger.
• A5.2, pre-action gating. A consequential action does not take effect unless its evidence commitment
is discharged, and the gating is causal and prior rather than documentary and subsequent.
• A5.3, divergence as signal. Any evidentiary status other than present-and-agreed is itself a declared
divergence event, logged and escalated, rather than defaulted to an assumption of either compliance or
violation.
• A5.4, operator-independent witness. At least one witness contributes evidence whose generation
and transmission channel the operator does not control, stated separately from A5.1 because A5.1 admits
a witness external to S but internal to the operator.
• A5.5, boundary declaration. The completeness boundary is declared before operation, and events
outside it are reported as outside rather than silently treated as covered.
The proposal also distinguishes five properties that informal discussion conflates: record integrity, provenance
and authenticity, observation completeness, semantic adequacy, and causal enforcement. That distinction is
independently valuable and this paper takes it as given.

5. What would count as a derivation
A clause is derivable if it follows from A1 through A4 applied to the evidence substrate, treating the ledger
and its witnesses as themselves a substrate with a surface, a set of callers, and a projection family. This
reflexive application is one the framework already performs, and it is the strongest available route to showing
that no new axiom is needed.
A clause is not derivable if a model exists satisfying A1 through A4 and violating the clause. Exhibiting
such a model is the operative test throughout. The test is the standard model-theoretic independence
criterion: a formula P is independent of a theory T exactly when neither P nor ¬P is provable from T,
demonstrated by exhibiting a model of T extended with the negation of P 3 ; the negative direction here uses
the contrapositive of soundness.
All proofs in this paper are finite model-theoretic paper proofs, verified by explicit clause-by-clause
enumeration over finite structures. They are not machine-checked. Formalization in a proof assistant remains
future work, and no claim of machine-checked verification is made anywhere in this paper.

6. Independence of evidence generation is not derivable (A5.1)
The canonical statement of A4 contains the sentence on which this turns: absence of a projection at a point
places that point outside A4’s scope per the joint-domain discipline, not inside it as a violation 1 . A4 is
therefore conditional on projections existing. It requires coherence where projections are defined and
their domains meet; it requires nothing about whether any projection is defined anywhere.
A5.1 is existential about existence: for each covered event, at least one S-independent observer has
produced evidence. That is the requirement A4 deliberately declines to make, and no reading of A4 supplies
it. This aligns with the standard notion of axiom independence via a witnessing structure3 .
Nor does A2. A2 requires a declared correspondence relation holding under a declared observer model
and context set. The observer model is a declaration, and nothing in A2 constrains what it may contain.
3


A system may declare its own logging as its observer model and satisfy A2 relative to that
declaration, which is exactly the configuration A5.1 forbids. Nor A3, whose subject is enumeration and
discharge of outcomes reaching callers. Nor A1, whose subject is version-to-version surface compatibility in
the Liskov-Wing subtyping sense4 .
6.1 The surviving axiom, stated
We state the survivor over a relation precise enough to distinguish independence from the acting substrate
(A5.1) from independence from the operator institution (A5.4). We deliberately do not define independence
as a mismatch of authority labels (auth(gen(r)) ̸= auth(S)): that is too weak, because authority domains
can overlap or nest, and labels can differ while S retains causal control of the evidence-generation act. We
define independence in terms of control, not labels.
Definition (WithinAuth). For an act x and a substrate S, WithinAuth(x, S) holds iff S can cause,
modify, suppress, reconfigure, or credential x: influence whether x occurs, what it records, whether
it is emitted, how it is configured, or under what identity it is authenticated, without an independently detectable violation. Intuitively, x lies within S’s authority exactly when S could bend or
fabricate x and no party outside S could tell.

Axiom 5 (Evidence independence). Let S be a declared substrate; E * a declared set of safetyrelevant event tokens or types as instantiated in operation; B a declared completeness boundary over
E * ; W a declared witness set; and L an evidence ledger. For every event e in E * that lies within B,
there exist a witness w in W and a record r in L such that r is evidence of e, the generation of r is
attributable to w, and the evidence-generation act is not within S’s authority: ¬WithinAuth(gen(r),
S).

WithinAuth is a primitive relation of the declared formal structure. Operationalizing "independently detectable" in a deployment inherits the authority regress acknowledged in the conclusion (every
constructible witness executes within someone’s authority) and is bounded in the joint-satisfiability model
of Section 7 only by the declared-external-authority device, not eliminated. Within the finite models the
relation is given by explicit assignment; in deployment it is an interpretation the declaration must fix and a
reviewer must contest.
Three further features are deliberate. The axiom stays at the acting-substrate level: WithinAuth is
quantified over S, the acting substrate, and not over the operator institution, so it does not accidentally
import A5.4. It makes the existential requirement explicit. And it carries the declared boundary B, which
A5.5 (Section 10) governs theorematically. Operator authority is kept separate: an operator may control
both S and the witness w, so that w is genuinely outside S’s control (satisfying A5) while still inside the
operator’s control (violating the A5.4 deployment condition of Section 11). Authority labels such as auth(·)
are used below only as illustrative assignments in the finite models, never as the definition of independence;
the definition is WithinAuth.

7. Joint satisfiability of A1-A4 + A5, verified clause by clause
Independence establishes that A5 is not redundant; joint satisfiability establishes that adding it does not
render the axiom set unsatisfiable. An unsatisfiable axiom set is trivially "consistent" with everything and
proves nothing, so this witness is required, not optional.
We distinguish two claims the earlier draft blurred: (1) axiom-set satisfiability, A1-A4 together with A5
(the single surviving axiom), which this paper discharges; and (2) proposal-bundle implementability,
coexistence of the full original five-clause proposal in one deployment profile, a deployment question this
paper does not claim.

Boundary discipline; loss and asynchrony do not relax B. The boundary B is fixed ex ante by event

within B lacks timely external evidence, because the external witness fails or its evidence arrives late, that
occurrence cannot be moved outside B post hoc. Two distinct statuses then hold, and the paper keeps
them separate. At the axiom level, A5 is UNSATISFIED (nonconforming) for that occurrence. At
the event-level adjudicative level, the verdict is UNKNOWN under Proposition 1: the occurrence is
neither defaulted to compliance nor to violation. Only events that the predeclared boundary excluded were
uncovered ab initio; nothing becomes uncovered retroactively. Accordingly, witness-unavailable is a handled
possible outcome under A3 (a typed incompleteness indicator, not a silent success), but a run in which it
occurs for an in-B event produces an A5 violation in that different run; it does not satisfy A5. M-plus
is a satisfiability witness precisely because its actual event e is a successful external-witness occurrence; the
unavailable branch is exhibited only to show A3 handling, not to claim A5. This axiom-set consistency
witness is distinct from a claim that the original five-clause proposal bundle is implementable; the latter is
not asserted here.

8. Causal gating is a proposed control principle, not an axiom (A5.2)
A5.2 is also not derivable, but the argument is structural rather than witness-based. A1 through A4 are
uniformly representational: each constrains what a system must record, declare, enumerate, or keep
coherent. Not one constrains what a system may do. A5.2 constrains action: a consequential action does
not take effect absent a discharged evidence commitment. No conjunction of representational requirements entails a constraint on action without an additional bridging premise, so A5.2 does not follow.
This is a type-level argument and requires no model.
The interesting consequence is what admitting the clause would do. A framework whose axioms are
all about honest representation is a diagnostic framework; one that also constrains action is a control
framework, with different burdens of proof and reviewer expectations. The flagship explicitly disclaims
prevention: it is "a contribution to evaluation integrity, oversight, and deployment monitoring ... it does not
supply a training-time method for preventing a model from becoming deceptively aligned"1 .
Adding causal gating to AX-SH would therefore change its diagnostic character. We treat this
not as a discovered fact about the program but as an explicit authorial architecture decision made in
this paper: AX-SH is to remain a diagnostic framework, and a causal gate does not belong in its axiom set.
This decision is the author’s, stated here so a reviewer can contest it directly (F-3).
6


Relocation, stated honestly. The proposal names a control architecture, "SHIELD," as the natural home
for A5.2. No citable SHIELD artifact is available for verification in the materials underlying this
paper. We therefore do not claim SHIELD already contains this gate. We state the relocation as a proposed
control principle:
Proposed SHIELD control principle (pre-action evidence gating). A consequential action may
acquire authority to take effect only after the evidence commitment specified for that action class has
been discharged at the gate that mediates execution.

A candidate control-layer derivation, to be developed if and where SHIELD is instantiated as a citable
artifact: authority to act is a function of attributability; attributability requires a recording relation; where
none holds, authority is not merely narrowed but undefined; fail-closed gating then follows as a consequence
rather than a stipulation. This connects to the established principle that a reference validation mechanism
must mediate access and be independently assessable7 . Until a SHIELD artifact exists in citable form, this
derivation is offered as the shape the relocation would take, not as an accomplished result.

9. Divergence as signal is a theorem plus an open requirement (A5.3)
9.1 A5.3a is a theorem (no-default)
Proposition 1 (No-default). For any covered event, missing, conflicting, or unverifiable evidence shall not be defaulted to either compliance or violation.
Proof. Two directions. Defaulting to violation treats an absent projection as an in-scope violation, contradicting A4’s statement that absence "places that point outside the axiom’s scope, not inside it as a
violation"1 . Defaulting to compliance asserts a surface claim with no substrate backing under the declared
correspondence relation, which by A2 is a surface-substrate divergence. Both defaults are forbidden, one by
A4 and one by A2. [QED]
A5.3a is thus a theorem from A2 and A4 and needs no new axiom. Its output for an unsupported occurrence
is exactly the event-level UNKNOWN verdict invoked in Section 7, which is distinct from the axiom-level
UNSATISFIED status of A5 for the same occurrence.
9.2 A5.3b is an open operational requirement (record/type/escalate)
A5.3b (residual requirement). Any evidentiary status other than present-and-agreed shall
itself be recorded, typed, and escalated according to declared handling rules.
This is a positive obligation A4 does not create: A4 places absence outside its scope, and placing
something outside scope is not the same as requiring it be recorded. The nearest route is A1’s append-only
discipline applied reflexively to the evidence ledger. But A1’s subject is a module’s public surface
across versions, and an evidentiary status is not a public surface in that sense. Treating the ledger’s
divergence record as an A1-eligible surface requires an additional bridging lemma the proposal gestures
at but does not prove.
We decline to present an analogy as a proof. A5.3b is retained as an additional operational requirement, not a theorem, and the bridging lemma is preserved as an explicit open obligation (UNKNOWN).
Should a clean A1 derivation be supplied (F-4), the clause becomes a theorem in full.

10. Boundary declaration is a theorem (A5.5)
7


Proposition 2 (Boundary declaration). If the observer model and context set relevant to
an evidence claim must be declared under A2, then (i) the coverage boundary B of that observer
model for A5-governed events must also be declared, and (ii) any claim that an A5 guarantee holds
for an event outside B is a surface claim unsupported by the declared substrate and therefore
violates A2.
Proof. (i) A2 requires the observer model to be declared1 . The completeness boundary is the coverage extent
of the observer model, specifying for which events the model produces evidence, and is therefore a component
of that declaration; omitting it leaves the observer model incompletely declared, so A2 is unsatisfied. (ii) An
uncovered event lies outside the declared context set K ; a claim that an A5 guarantee holds there asserts that
the correspondence relation holds outside the K under which it was declared, a surface-substrate divergence
by A2. Both halves follow from A2 alone. [QED]
Temporal boundary handling. Every deployment adopting A5 has a past with no witness-authored
evidence. Pre-adoption events belong in the uncovered set by declaration, and must never be reconstructed from subject-emitted logs; reconstruction would reimport exactly the self-attestation A5 exists
to displace. The declared boundary B must therefore carry an explicit temporal floor. A5.5 is a theorem;
prove it in the monograph, do not axiomatize it.

11. Operator-independence is a measured deployment condition (A5.4)
A5.4 is not derivable, and it is not the same kind of claim as A5.1. A5.1 says an observer outside the acting
system’s control must exist, checkable in principle by asking whether WithinAuth(gen(r), S) holds (can S
cause, modify, suppress, reconfigure, or credential the act without an independently detectable violation?).
A5.4 says at least one witness must sit outside the operator’s authority, a claim about how verification
authority is distributed among institutions. The two come apart precisely because an operator may control
both S and the witness: such a witness is outside S’s control (A5 satisfied) yet inside the operator’s control
(A5.4 unsatisfied), which is why A5.4 is a separate, institutional condition and not part of the axiom.
No structural property of a substrate entails this, and no architecture guarantees it. Any check
the operator runs on a witness’s independence is a check the operator could have arranged, and any attestation is one the operator could have procured. This is the auditing discipline that self-arranged verification is
not independent verification: independence and impartiality of the verifying body are institutional properties, per ISO/IEC 17029’s requirements on validation and verification bodies9 and the reference-monitor and
independent-assessment concepts in NIST SP 800-537 . We explicitly do not claim that architecture
can guarantee operator independence. A5.4 is a deployment condition, not a derived theorem.
11.1 The measured restatement (markedness)
Extend the evidence relation from Ev(O, e) to Ev(O, e, A), where A records the authority under which
observer O operated at attestation time. Per covered event class c, report the out-of-authority attestation
coverage:
Covout (c) = | { e in c : there exist O,A such that Ev(O,e,A) and A ̸= Aop } | / | { e in c : e
covered } |
separately reporting Covsame (c) and Covunk (c). Evidence with no recorded authority is UNKNOWN
and is never counted as independent.
What this buys. Under the existential formulation, a deployment with one out-of-authority witness covering one of forty event classes is indistinguishable from one covering all forty. Under the coverage formulation
the difference is a number. What it does not buy. Independence does not become engineerable; a recorded
authority attribution can be false, and the gain is only that falsity now requires a specific, attributable, signed
misstatement rather than a silence.
8


Self-attestation, stated precisely. Self-attestation may be evidence; ISO conformity practice recognizes
first-party declarations as a legitimate attestation instrument10 . But it cannot independently discharge
an evidence-independence requirement: a first-party declaration and a third-party attestation are
distinct instruments, and one does not substitute for the other.
Non-apportionment (explicit deployment rule, with a test). The presence of an independent witness
adds a party who answers and subtracts nothing from the operator’s own account. Without this rule, A5.4
reads as satisfiable by procurement. We state non-apportionment as an explicit deployment rule, not
a derived theorem. Falsification/test condition (F-9): if a deployment treats a procured out-ofauthority attestation as discharging the operator’s own account without a residual operator-side obligation,
the rule has failed operationally and must be strengthened.

12. The markedness discipline
The move made three times in this paper, recording attesting authority (Section 11), naming the evidenceledger bridging gap (Section 9.2), and recording the signing scheme per ledger entry (Section 13), is one
discipline, named explicitly:
Markedness discipline. When a relevant property cannot be directly verified, record whether its
evidence is marked (the relevant attribute is recorded) or unmarked; report the marked and unmarked
fractions per class; and preserve unmarked cases as UNKNOWN, never defaulting them to the favorable value.

We characterize this as internal methodological consistency across the program’s own methods, not as
external proof that the discipline is a general law. The convergence is across parts of a single author’s corpus;
it is evidence that the program applies its method consistently, not that the method has been independently
discovered elsewhere. Overstating it would itself be the surface claim the framework exists to detect.

13. The cryptographic boundary the proposal does not declare
The extension’s properties do not survive a quantum adversary uniformly, and the split runs against the
extension.
Record integrity is more robust than attribution, with the security notion stated exactly.
Against a quantum adversary, hash security must be split by the specific notion. For a 256-bit hash,
preimage and second-preimage resistance fall to about 2128 work under Grover’s quadratic speedup, while
generic collision finding has query complexity about 285 under the Brassard-Hoyer-Tapp bound where it
applies (and remains near the classical 2128 once realistic memory and parallelism costs are counted)5 . We
therefore do not call 128-bit a single "collision-level" figure, and we do not claim categorically that
"record integrity survives quantum"; we claim that hash-based integrity components are materially more
robust under known quantum attacks than classical public-key signatures, with the notion (preimage vs
collision) named wherever a number is used.
Attribution is quantum-fragile. Every mechanism the proposal uses to attribute evidence rests on digital
signatures. Classical schemes (RSA, ECDSA, EdDSA) are on NIST’s quantum-vulnerable migration path6 .
If the scheme is broken, Axiom 5 and the A5.4 deployment condition fail together, because both depend
on knowing who attested.
Standardized post-quantum signatures. NIST finalized FIPS 204 (ML-DSA, from CRYSTALS-Dilithium)
and FIPS 205 (SLH-DSA, from SPHINCS+) on August 13, 202411,12 . ML-DSA is NIST’s primary standardized post-quantum signature standard; SLH-DSA is a hash-based alternative whose security
rests solely on hash-function properties. We do not call SLH-DSA categorically preferable. The tradeoff is

9


explicit: SLH-DSA inherits the conservative assumption of the integrity layer but at the cost of substantially larger signatures and lower throughput, practical for low-frequency root-anchoring, costly for a
high-throughput evidence ledger, where ML-DSA’s smaller signatures may be the better engineering choice.
Long-lived attribution requires active protection. We do not claim entries are "retroactively forgeable"
unconditionally. The conditional risk is the recognized "trust now, forge later" hazard: if historical attestation validity relies only on now-broken classical signatures and lacks migrated protections, an adversary
may later fabricate apparently valid historical attestations. Later key or algorithm compromise can
undermine standalone historical attribution unless the record is protected by trusted timestamps and
evidence-record renewal and re-anchoring in the sense of long-term archival Evidence Record Syntax13 and
long-term preservation policy for trust service providers, whose explicit aim is to keep a signature validatable
even after the signing key is compromised or cryptographic attacks become feasible on the signature or hash
algorithm14 , periodic re-anchoring of the ledger root, retained validation material, and crypto-agility with
the signing scheme recorded per entry, consistent with NIST key-management guidance on cryptoperiods
and algorithm transition8 .
Recording the scheme per entry is the markedness discipline (Section 12) in its cryptographic instance.
Falsification condition (F-8): if witness attestation cannot be migrated to a post-quantum scheme with
per-entry crypto-agility and periodic re-anchoring, the long-term verifiability Axiom 5 depends on is not
achievable and the historical-record guarantee fails.

14. Consequences for the monograph, if adopted
Adopting Axiom 5 is canon-touching, with three consequences that should be stated and not executed
here. The mutual-independence theorem becomes a five-way rather than four-way claim; the countermodel
of Section 6.2 supplies one additional case. The finite-consistency witness gains an external observer; the
construction of Section 7 is the candidate. The definitional-adequacy judgment set is expected to be unchanged, because no case turns on evidence independence, but this must be verified case by case, not
asserted.
None of this should be undertaken until the extension is settled. Corpus-ontology work is sequenced
after publication and adjudication of this paper: a canon revision restarts an audit clock on a deposited
artifact, and doing that for an extension still under examination would repeat a sequencing error the program
has made once.

16. Falsification conditions
Every condition is registered in the open, under the framework’s own first axiom turned on itself: a registered
prediction is preserved; a later revision is an annotation, never a silent replacement.

10


F-1. If a competent reader derives Axiom 5 (A5.1) from A1-A4, the central positive result fails and the
extension is redundant. This is the condition most likely to fire; a reviewer should attempt it first.
F-2. If the independence countermodel (Section 6.2) is defective, if the exhibited structure does not in fact
satisfy all four existing axioms, the independence claim fails. Discharged by the exhibited paper model of
Section 6.2; remains live against defects or stronger semantics.
F-3. If A1-A4 can be shown to constrain action rather than only representation, the relocation of A5.2 fails
and the authorial architecture decision of Section 8 is contested.
F-4. If A5.3b derives cleanly from A1 via the bridging lemma of Section 9.2, that clause is a theorem in full.
F-5. If out-of-authority attestation coverage proves no more determinate in practice than the existential it
replaces, Section 11’s contribution is nil.
F-6. If a witness arrangement can be exhibited that is verifiably outside the operator’s authority by construction rather than declaration, the non-structural verdict on A5.4 fails and A5.4 is axiom-shaped after
all. This would be good news and the paper welcomes it.
F-7. If the joint-satisfiability construction (Section 7) cannot be exhibited, the axiom set A1-A4+A5 is not
shown consistent and the extension should not be adopted regardless of the other results. Discharged by the
exhibited paper model of Section 7; remains live against defects or stronger semantics.
F-8. (Cryptographic.) Failure to migrate attestation to a post-quantum scheme with per-entry crypto-agility
and re-anchoring defeats the long-term verifiability Axiom 5 depends on.
F-9. (Non-apportionment.) If a procured out-of-authority attestation is treated as discharging the operator’s
own account, the non-apportionment deployment rule has failed operationally.
F-10. (Positive inconsistency.) If a model can be exhibited satisfying A1-A4 and provably precluding
any A5-compliant observer, joint satisfiability fails outright and the extension is inconsistent, not merely
unshown, a stronger and more damaging result than mere non-derivability.
F-2 and F-7 are discharged by the exhibited paper models of Sections 6.2 and 7, and both remain
live against defects or stronger semantics: a reader who finds a defect in either construction, or who
adopts a stronger model-theoretic semantics under which the constructions fail, reopens them.

17. Limitations
• The two proofs (Propositions 1 and 2) and the two constructions (Sections 6.2, 7) are finite paper proofs
a reader can check by enumeration; they are not formalized in a proof assistant, and that formalization
is outstanding work.
• WithinAuth is a primitive relation of the declared formal structure; its deployment interpretation inherits
the authority regress and is bounded, not eliminated, by the declared-external-authority device (Section
6.1).
• No SHIELD artifact is available for verification; the A5.2 relocation is stated as a proposed control
principle, with its control-layer derivation offered as a shape only.
• The paper examines a proposal produced by a collaborating instance under the author’s direction; the
author is therefore not a disinterested reader, and F-1 is where external readers should start.
• Nothing here establishes that evidence independence, however axiomatized, is achievable in a deployed
system. The assurance ladder’s top rung has no public instantiation. That remains UNKNOWN.

18. Conclusion

11


The proposal is right about the gap. Four axioms about honest representation can all hold over a record that
omits what matters, because none requires that anyone other than the acting system produce the record.
Closing that gap takes one axiom, not five clauses. Evidence for a covered event must come from an
observer that does not execute within the acting system’s authority, and that requirement is independent of
the existing four, with a finite countermodel to prove it and a finite joint-satisfiability witness to show the
enlarged set is consistent.
The rest of the proposal is not wasted. Causal gating is relocated, as an explicit architecture decision, to a
proposed control principle. Operator-independence becomes reportable when restated as measured coverage.
Boundary declaration and the no-default prohibition are theorems; the recording/escalation obligation is a
preserved open requirement. And the axiom carries a bound stated with it: every witness that can be
built executes within someone’s authority. Evidence independence narrows the gap between what a
system says and what a record can show; it does not close it, and a framework claiming otherwise would be
making exactly the surface claim it exists to detect.
