## Part XI: Cross-Corpus Synthesis -- The Seven Structural Discoveries

### 11.1 Discovery 1: Shannon's Boundary Is Empirically Locatable

The corpus's single most important contribution. Shannon established the theoretical boundary between signal and noise. FATIMA posits an analogous boundary between syntactic integrity and semantic integrity. The XZ Protocol located it empirically: it is at the self-referential surface -- where the artifact makes claims about its own verification properties.

Below the boundary: hashes, seals, manifests, test suites, CI gates. All verifiable. All automated. Above the boundary: "this repair landed," "this metric measures what we claim," "this gate covers the surface we say it covers." Not verifiable from within.

### 11.2 Discovery 2: The Goedel Sentence Operates Empirically

The corpus demonstrates that Goedel's incompleteness theorem is not merely a mathematical curiosity -- it operates as an empirical constraint on real verification systems. A bounded verification instrument that includes claims about its own verification properties cannot verify those claims from within. This is not a proof (Discovery 7); it is a 25-round empirical observation. But the observation is consistent and non-diminishing.

Bearer 4 (Goedel confinement) = FALSE at every version from v0.63 through v0.74. The defect migrates but does not vanish. Every repair opens new surface. The system cannot grade its own homework.

### 11.3 Discovery 3: Defect Migration Is Non-Monotonic

The intuition is that verification should narrow the defect surface over time. The empirical finding is that it does not -- not monotonically. R26 returns to code-layer defects after R22 declared the code layer clean. R31 returns to temporal integrity after R29 addressed it. The dominant locus shifts upward (code -> cross-layer -> temporal -> methodology -> prose), but individual rounds can regress.

The migration is structural, not random. Defects move to wherever the most recent repair is NOT: fix the code, defects appear in the prose about the code. Fix the prose, defects appear in the claims about the prose. Fix the claims, defects appear in the metrics that measure the claims. This is the Goedel sentence in motion.

### 11.4 Discovery 4: Gated Claims Stay Clean

The operational form of Shannon's Boundary. Across R22-R31 (the rounds where the code layer is clean), every defect that recurs does so at a locus no gate covers. Gated claims -- those verified by build.py, CI-1R, manifest gates, version gates -- stay clean once fixed. Ungated prose claims carry Class A every round.

This means: the boundary between verifiable and unverifiable is operationally identical to the boundary between gated and ungated. Extend the gate, and the new surface is clean -- but new ungated surface is created by the gate's own claims about what it covers.

### 11.5 Discovery 5: Evidence Independence Is an Architectural Axiom

The BSSG kernel does not inspect evidence. It makes its five-conjunct decision based on metadata: does the contract match? Is the effect type valid? Is the target allowed? Is the witness healthy? Is the evidence current? The evidence itself is never examined by the gate.

This is architecturally necessary. If the gate inspected evidence, the gate could be compromised by adversarial evidence construction. Evidence independence makes the gate immune to this attack vector -- but it also means the gate cannot detect evidence fabrication. This tradeoff is explicitly disclosed (Bearer 6, axiom disclosure).

### 11.6 Discovery 6: Multi-Model Audit Reveals Vendor-Specific Blind Spots

No single AI model can find all defects. Each vendor family has systematic blind spots. The corpus demonstrates this across five families over 31 rounds. The strongest signal comes from fresh seats (no prior context) of a different family than the producer.

But seat mis-routing weakens this: when seats dispatched to one family are executed by another (systematic across R22-R31), the cross-vendor independence that makes multi-model audit valuable is compromised. The findings are often stronger (the producer's own family finds what the producer missed), but the independence claim cannot be discharged.

### 11.7 Discovery 7: The Empirical Series Does Not Establish Formal Impossibility

The XZ Protocol's 25-round streak does not prove that verification convergence is formally impossible. It demonstrates that a specific bounded instrument, over 25 rounds, with specific model families, consistently found Class A defects. The Goedel analogy is empirical, not formal -- the strongest publishable result (per the empirical paper's own adversarial audit) is:

"102 extracted Class A findings occur in 19 of 25 R7-R31 rounds, with non-monotonic migration across protocol, CI, methodology, provenance, and cross-layer surfaces."

This is weaker than "structural honesty cannot verify itself" but stronger than any prior empirical result in the verification literature.
